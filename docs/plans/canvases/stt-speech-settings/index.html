YAAT-1602: Settings > Speech, the new speech-recognition block

Two layouts for the same controls: the STT model (rasr-parakeet v2 fp32 by default, int8, and Whisper until YAAT-1574), the mode (accurate by default, fast), the device (CPU or GPU) and the GPU runtime download. Each lane shows two states: a fresh install, where nothing is downloaded and the card is too old for CUDA 13, and a configured machine, where fp32 is ready and the GPU was just chosen, so a restart is pending. Only the speech-recognition part of the section is drawn. The LLM, push-to-talk, telemetry and TTS groups below it are unchanged.

The driver and compute-capability figures are placeholders. The real minimums are taken from NVIDIA's CUDA 13 release notes when the check is built.

C. Round 2: lane A's rows, with a ComboBox that opens into a scrollable card list

This answers round 1 ("Combobox with custom control for expansion that shows a scrollable card list rather than just names"). The closed picker reads as one row, as in lane A. Opening it shows a card for each model, with its size, where it runs best, its download state and, once YAAT-1604 lands, the measured RAM and latency for the chosen mode and device. Picking a card closes the list. Download and Delete stay in the row under the picker, for the selected model. Whisper is the last card, marked legacy, until YAAT-1574 removes it.

This uses the same controls as today's Whisper and LLM groups: a model ComboBox with a Download / Delete row, then radio rows for mode and device, then the GPU runtime as a sub-group. B. Model cards with segmented toggles

Each rasr model is a card showing its size, its memory and where it runs best, and the card is the selection. Mode and device are segmented toggles, and the GPU runtime gets its own card. The cards are where the in-app benchmark (YAAT-1604) puts measured RAM and latency per model and mode. Whisper sits under the cards as a legacy link.
