Solo training: the traffic and field advisory from the flyout

In solo training the sim refuses RTIS {callsign} and bare RTIS (“Use RTIS <clock> <miles> <direction> <type> [altitude] in solo training”, NavigationCommandHandler.cs:1530), and bare RFIS (“Use RFIS <clock> <miles>”, :1446). The student is the controller: they must give the advisory as they would say it, and the sim matches it to traffic and scores it. This page draws three ways for the student to build that call from the Report traffic in sight… flyout (the lane B layout the owner chose) without typing the whole thing, and the same for the field advisory from Report field in sight….

The situation: N52TW, a PA28 VFR inbound to KOAK from the southeast at 2,000 ft, heading 300; nothing reported in sight yet. Seven aircraft are airborne within 7 NM; N9152M is on a left base for 28L, N738SP on a left downwind, SWA2287 on final 30, and N22RX is over the Oakland Coliseum reporting point (VPCOL). Positions follow each row's clock and distance; they are illustrative, not from a recording.

Each lane draws, in order: the flyout open over the radar with an aircraft picked (ring and bearing line), its advisory filled and what Enter sends; the pattern-leg form near the field; the field advisory; then a refused call, a call that describes other traffic, a form the aircraft cannot take, an incomplete call, and the empty state. Everything is live: pick rows, step values, switch forms, press Enter; the line under each lane says what would be sent and what the sim would answer. The answers imitate the sim's matcher on the drawn positions.

Carried from the owner's rulings (drawn as given, not as lanes)

  • Lane B layout. Report traffic in sight… and Follow traffic… are two flyouts on one layout: title line, a glyph box row whose command column previews what Enter sends, a separator, a 300 px list of airborne traffic, nearest first. The advisory sits between the box and the list.
  • Follow (not drawn here): rows not reported in sight are greyed, with an RPO-only Force that sends FOLLOWF; Force is absent in solo, so in solo a follow always needs a reported advisory first.
  • Radar cue on hover and keyboard focus: a ring on the target and a dashed bearing line from the selected aircraft. Here the picked aircraft stays ringed while its advisory is open; lane C rings the aircraft the typed call resolves to.
  • RPO mode is unchanged: rows send RTIS {callsign} as the part D mock draws. The advisory builder replaces that only in solo training.

What the sim does with the call (static reads, not run)

  • Tolerant matching. The structured call resolves to the best-fitting airborne aircraft within clock ±2 h, ±2 NM, direction ±1 octant, altitude ±500 ft; it scores clean within 1 h, 1 NM, the exact direction and 100 ft, else adds an “advisory imprecise” coaching note (TrafficAdvisoryMatcher.cs:68-83, COMMANDS.md:1546-1548). Nose: ±1 octant, ±2 NM; pattern leg: leg, side and runway exact, ±2 NM; over a point: within 2 NM of it.
  • The call can land on other traffic. The lowest weighted error wins, so a call that fits another aircraft better resolves to it, not to the aircraft the student meant; only the pilot's reply tells them.
  • Out of tolerance is refused at once: “Unable, no aircraft matches traffic 5 o'clock, 4 mile(s), Wbound, SR22, 3,500” (TrafficAdvisoryMatcher.cs:151; nose :262, pattern :346, landmark :411). The {Direction}bound text reads “Wbound”, “NWbound” (Describe, :481-485): a nit.
  • The field advisory is not checked. RFIS <clock> <miles> runs the field acquisition and echoes the phrase (NavigationCommandHandler.cs:1522-1526); the evaluator records field proof for any values (SoloTrainingEvaluator.cs:142-145). RFIS 6 30 proves it as well as RFIS 12 5. aviation review if it should be graded.
  • No dry run exists. The matcher works on AircraftState in the sim; a check before sending (lane C) needs it run against the client's snapshot or a server round trip. needs building

Open decisions for the owner

  1. 1. How the student builds the call.A a chip line pre-filled from the picked row, stepped and sent with Enter · B a builder (clock dial, miles, direction pad, type, altitude) seeded from the pointed row · C the short form typed in the box, Tab taking each part from the pointed row, a live check naming the aircraft it resolves to.
  2. 2. Pre-filled values (the page switch).a the true ones: easy, teaches less; the Session Report scores every call clean · b left for the student: clock, miles and direction blank, type and altitude filled (read off the data block); rows and the bearing label stop printing clock and miles · c a solo-session setting (“Coach”/“Check ride”) choosing between them.
  3. 3. Check before sending.a none: the pilot's reply and the Session Report grade it (A and B as drawn) · b a live check under the box (C as drawn; needs the matcher on the client or a dry-run call) · c only after a refusal, naming the part that is out.
  4. 4. On a refusal.a the flyout stays open with the sim's reply, the values kept for a fix (as drawn; the client waits for the command result) · b it closes as on success, the reply in the terminal as today.
  5. 5. Forms offered.a all four, Radar · Nose · Pattern · Over, each greyed with its reason when the aircraft cannot take it (as drawn) · b Radar only · c Radar, plus Pattern when the aircraft is in a pattern leg.
  6. 6. The field advisory in solo.a Report field in sight becomes a flyout with the lane's clock and miles, in solo only (as drawn; RPO keeps the one-click bare RFIS) · b also in RPO. And: should the sim grade RFIS clock and miles as it grades RTIS (sim change, aviation review)?
  7. 7. Which aircraft stays ringed.a the pointed row, else the picked one while its advisory is open (A, B as drawn) · b the pointed row only (the ruling's letter) · c in C, the aircraft the typed call resolves to (as drawn), which can differ from the one pointed at.
