Solo traffic advisory: how the live check behaves

The owner chose lane C of the solo advisory mock: the student types the call in the Report traffic in sight flyout as they would say it (2 4 w sr22), the lane B builder sits below for anyone who would rather click, and a live check under the box names the aircraft the sim would resolve the call to and grades each part. This page asks the four things left open about that check. The situation is the earlier page's: N52TW, a PA28 VFR inbound to KOAK at 2,000 ft heading 300, with N22RX (SR22, westbound, 3,500) at 2 o'clock, 4 miles. Positions are illustrative, not from a recording.

Each lane draws the scope excerpt above the flyout rather than under it, so lanes fit side by side; in the app the flyout opens over the scope beside the aircraft menu. The first two sections are animated: press Replay, drag the time bar, or open the page with #t=1200 to freeze both at 1.2 s (#t=1200&t2=9000 sets the second section separately).

Carried from the owner's rulings (drawn as given, not as lanes)

  • Say it, with the builder below. The box takes the short form; the lane B builder (clock dial, miles, direction pad, type, altitude) sits under it in solo. It is folded to one line here because no decision on this page touches it.
  • The check runs on the client, against the client's aircraft list (MainViewModel.Aircraft), which the server refreshes once per sim-second. It imitates the sim's matcher: tolerance gates, the weighted error and the clean bands.
  • Its own solo setting, default Off. With it off the flyout has no check line and the pilot's reply grades the call; every lane here draws it On.
  • RPO mode keeps the traffic list whose rows send RTIS {callsign}; nothing on this page applies there.

1 · Check cadence: when the line re-grades while the student types

When does the check run on what has been typed so far?

All four lanes type the same call at the same moments: 2 4 w sr22, about 140 ms between letters and half a second of thought between parts, then Enter at 4.3 s. Lane D also presses its preview key (drawn as Ctrl+Space, a placeholder) at 3.5 s. The bar under each lane marks keystrokes (grey) and each run of the check (coloured dots). The switch below sets what lanes B to D show while the text has moved past the last check.

2 · Snapshot drift: the traffic moves while the student pauses

Does the line re-grade when the aircraft moves, or only when the text changes?

The student types 2 4 w sr22 while N22RX is at 2 o'clock, then pauses. N52TW turns left at standard rate, so N22RX drifts round to 3 o'clock; the client sees each step at its once-a-second update. The student presses Enter one second after the turn ends. Both lanes check on every keystroke while typing, so only the drift differs. The bar marks client updates (faint), check runs (dots) and, in lane B, the stretch the grade is stale (amber).

A drift to 3 o'clock stays inside the matcher's clean band (one hour either way), so only the clock mark's detail changes; the switch below continues the turn to 240 so N22RX reaches 4 o'clock, where the grade turns imprecise.

3 · Which aircraft stays ringed when the call resolves elsewhere

The student pointed at N811CP but typed a call that fits N4417Q better. What does the scope ring?

The student clicked N811CP (C182, 4 o'clock, 7 miles, southbound) and typed 5 6 nw c182 020. N811CP is southbound, three octants from northwest, so the matcher's direction gate drops it; N4417Q (C182, 6 o'clock, 5 miles, northwestbound, 2,000) fits inside every clean band and the check resolves the call to it. Static: no animation.

4 · Where the field advisory lives

Where does the student give RFIS <clock> <miles>?

The field advisory takes the same say-it box (12 5: KOAK at 12 o'clock, 5 miles) but has no live check: the sim runs the field acquisition and echoes the phrase without testing clock or miles (NavigationCommandHandler.cs:1649-1653), and the Session Report counts any values as field proof (SoloTrainingEvaluator.cs:142). A faint “not checked” takes the check line's place in every lane. Static: no animation.

Open decisions for the owner

  1. 1. Check cadence.a every keystroke · b 300 ms after typing stops · c at a part boundary (Space or Tab) · d only on a preview key. And for b to d: between checks the line shows the last grade dimmed, nothing, or “checking…” (the switch in section 1).
  2. 2. Drift while typing.a re-grade on each aircraft-list update as well as on each keystroke · b re-grade on text changes only; dim the grade and mark it stale once a part it graded reads differently.
  3. 3. Which aircraft stays ringed.a the pointed row keeps the ring, the resolved aircraft gets a second, dashed ring · b the ring moves to the resolved aircraft · c the ring stays on the pointed row and the check line names the other aircraft in the warning colour (the warning text can go with a or b too).
  4. 4. Field advisory placement.a one flyout with a Traffic · Field toggle · b its own strip entry and small flyout · c a section below the traffic list in the same flyout.
The check imitates TrafficAdvisoryMatcher.ResolveStructuredTrafficTarget: gates of 2 h, 2 NM, one octant and 500 ft; clean within 1 h, 1 NM, the exact direction and 100 ft (TrafficAdvisoryMatcher.cs:65-75); the lowest weighted error wins (:134-135); a type matches on its first three letters (:514). Spoken lines follow CommandDescriber.FormatTrafficAdvisoryPhrase (:2750) and FormatFieldAdvisoryPhrase (:2865). The scope, flyout and glyphs are the solo advisory mock's drawing (../yaat-569-d-solo-advisory/index.html), not YAAT's renderer.
