Taxi to runway tabs: five open choices

Every lane draws the settled first-batch lane B: a tab per runway end (the assigned end, then the room's departure ends, then the rest in runway order), the chosen end's entries as titles (RW badge, name, reason, runway left ahead, via, distance), and every variant its own glyph row with the command it sends. Variant labels shorten “For Departure 30” to “Departure 30” as the first-batch drawing does. Each artboard asks one open question; the first lane is the one this mock recommends.

Data is real: UAL455, A320 (7,185 ft takeoff distance in AircraftProfiles.json), at KOAK gate 25 and KSFO gate F12, on the bundled layouts (oak.geojson, sfo.geojson). Entries follow GroundViewModel.EntryRowsFor's rules (full-length bar within 300 ft of the threshold, intersections leaving at least the takeoff distance, opposite-threshold bars left out, crossings named by the crossed runway's first end, so KSFO's 28L is “10R”). Routes and distances come from a shortest path over the layout's centrelines measured for this mock, not from the client's pathfinder, so a via or a distance can differ from what YAAT offers by a taxiway or a few hundred feet. Click rows and tabs; Q1's flyouts take the keyboard.

Row templates follow the first-batch mock (docs/plans/canvases/quick-command-flyouts/, Taxi to runway lane B) and MenuCommandRow.cs / MenuGlyphRow.cs: Inter text, commands in JetBrains Mono at 70%, the Taxi to runway glyph at 22 px in the ground colour, RW badges outlined in #E8A33D, the Fluent dark menu surface, the 480 px cap of MenuCommandRow.InlineRowMaxWidth. The page loads no web fonts, so it draws in Segoe UI and Consolas where Inter and JetBrains Mono are not installed, and the measured widths are those of the fonts it drew with. The pointer, the dashed notes and the measured-width labels are mock annotations, not app UI. The spinner delays (about a second) are mock timing; the real search time is unmeasured.
