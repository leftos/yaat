# Speech pipeline greenfield: state of the art and options

Date: 2026-10-05. Linear: YAAT-343 (Rethink the speech pipeline greenfield with state-of-the-art STT). Sources were read on 2026-10-04; each citation gives its URL and, where the source carries one, its publication or release date. Today's pipeline: [`../speech-recognition-pipeline.md`](../speech-recognition-pipeline.md). Baseline numbers: [`2026-09-28-atc-ouroboros-baseline.md`](2026-09-28-atc-ouroboros-baseline.md) and [`../plans/stt-rule-gaps.md`](../plans/stt-rule-gaps.md).

## Summary

- The current pipeline fails mostly on **entities** (fix names, SID names, callsigns on short clips) and on **run-to-run noise** (two runs of one seed differed in 104 of 200 transcripts). Both are addressable without a new model: the Whisper runtime YAAT uses (LM-Kit `SpeechToText`) exposes no temperature, fallback, beam, seed or token-probability control, and whisper.cpp's default temperature fallback re-samples at higher temperatures on uncertain audio, which is a documented source of non-deterministic output. VICE turns that fallback off.
- **VICE**, the open-source TRACON trainer, ships a working design that YAAT can copy in approach (not in code: VICE is GPL-3.0, YAAT is MIT): ATC fine-tuned Whisper (base/small/medium, q5_0, 55-539 MB) on whisper.cpp with Vulkan, a per-PTT prompt built from the on-frequency aircraft's fixes, approaches, SIDs/STARs and callsigns in tail-priority order, temperature fallback off, and a local scored-template parser (Jaro-Winkler + Double Metaphone, beam search over segmentations, aircraft-state validation) that answers "say again" when unsure. VICE tried a cloud LLM for parsing and replaced it with that local parser.
- The open-model frontier moved to **Conformer/transducer models** (NVIDIA Parakeet-TDT 0.6B v2/v3, CC-BY-4.0) and **Conformer+LLM decoders** (Canary-Qwen-2.5B, Qwen3-ASR). Parakeet runs on CPU through sherpa-onnx, which YAAT already references, and sherpa-onnx gained per-stream **hotword biasing for NeMo transducers** in February 2026. ATC fine-tunes of Parakeet-TDT exist (5.99 % WER on the same ATC test split where the larger Whisper-large-v3 ATC fine-tune reports 6.5 %).
- The research on ATC is consistent: measure at **command level** (DLR's command recognition / error / rejection rates), use the scenario's **context** (active callsigns, fixes) both in decoding and after it, and resolve entities against that context. Surveillance-list callsign boosting raised callsign accuracy by 28-54 points absolute in the ATCO2-era papers.
- Recommendation: rebuild the **text-to-command stage and the evaluation first** (scored, context-resolved, deterministic, with rejection), fix decoding determinism, then run a **model bake-off** (Whisper-ATC with dynamic prompt vs Parakeet-TDT ATC fine-tune with per-PTT hotwords) under command-level metrics before choosing the acoustic model. An audio-LLM path (Gemma 4 E4B audio input with a grammar) is a research spike, not the base.

## Where the current pipeline loses

Read from the repo docs, not re-measured here:

- Baseline 157/200 PASS at batch 2; the first baseline was 153/200 with mean WER 19.9 % on clean Piper TTS audio ([`../plans/stt-rule-gaps.md`](../plans/stt-rule-gaps.md)).
- The largest STT-led failure group is programmed fixes misheard despite the biasing prompt (ALTAM → "ultima"/"ultramarve", CEPIN → "SE"/"7C"), then callsigns lost on short transmissions, then short words (IDENT, hold direction) ([`2026-09-28-atc-ouroboros-baseline.md`](2026-09-28-atc-ouroboros-baseline.md)).
- The Whisper prompt is **static** (rule literals, number vocabulary, NATO alphabet) and already over whisper.cpp's 224-token cap; no scenario fixes, SIDs or callsigns reach it today, despite the plan's assumption that programmed fixes feed it ([`../speech-recognition-pipeline.md`](../speech-recognition-pipeline.md), "Biasing Prompt").
- Two batch-2 runs of the same seed differed in 104 of 200 transcripts (YAAT-225). The eval code attributes trial variance to "GPU nondeterminism" (`tools/Yaat.SpeechSandbox/EvalRunner.cs:202`); the decoding settings that would explain it are not visible through LM-Kit (see below).
- A sandbox probe of stock Parakeet-TDT 0.6B v2 int8 (greedy, no hotwords, no ATC fine-tune) scored 68 % WER on the two real clips then in the corpus and was shelved (commit 6f7329254, 2026-07-30). Two clips is not a verdict on the model family.

## Findings: ATC domain adaptation

Datasets and fine-tunes:

- **ATCO2**: 4 h transcribed test data plus 5000+ h pseudo-transcribed ([Jzuluaga/atco2_corpus_1h](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Jzuluaga/atco2_corpus_1h)). **UWB-ATCC**: ~20 h, Czech airspace, 8 kHz, **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** ([Jzuluaga/uwb_atcc](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Jzuluaga/uwb_atcc)). Both are European, ICAO phraseology; neither is FAA phraseology or US accents.
- **jacktol/ATC-ASR-Dataset** (2025-05-28) combines UWB-ATCC and the ATCO2 1 h subset ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/jacktol/ATC-ASR-Dataset)). On its test split: jacktol Whisper-medium.en fine-tune 15.08 % WER (YAAT's current default, now marked deprecated by its author) ([card](https://huggingface.co/jacktol/whisper-medium.en-fine-tuned-for-ATC)); jacktol Whisper-large-v3 fine-tune 6.5 % (2025-08-24, MIT) ([card](https://huggingface.co/jacktol/whisper-large-v3-finetuned-for-ATC)); **Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 fine-tune 5.99 %** (2025-10-13, MIT) ([qenneth/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-finetuned-for-ATC](https://huggingface.co/qenneth/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-finetuned-for-ATC)). All three were trained on NC-SA data while declaring MIT.
- **Whisper-ATC** (van Doorn et al., ICRAT 2024, TU Delft): 13.5 % WER on ATCO2, 1.17 % on ATCOSIM (random split), region-specific data improved real-world WER by up to 60 % ([paper](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/218298256/ICRAT2024_paper_83.pdf); [jlvdoorn/WhisperATC](https://github.com/jlvdoorn/WhisperATC)). Cross-dataset results are much worse: the same group's large-v3 ATCO2 model scored 37.6 % on ATCO2 and 55 % on a Southeast-Asian set in a 2025 comparison ([arXiv 2502.20311, 2025-02-27](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20311v1)). Domain and accent match matter more than model size.

Contextual biasing:

- **Surveillance-list boosting** (ATCO2 project): boosting the n-grams of callsigns present at the utterance time gave up to +28.4 points callsign accuracy ([Nigmatulina et al., arXiv 2108.12156](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.12156)); adding a text-side re-ranking against the list reached +53.7 points ([arXiv 2202.03725, ICASSP 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.03725)); a BERT model given the surveillance list multiplied callsign accuracy up to four times on noisy transcripts and degraded as the list grew, so lists should stay as short as the context allows ([Blatt et al., arXiv 2204.06309](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.06309)).
- **Whisper initial prompt**: whisper.cpp keeps only the last ~223 tokens of the prompt, so the order of what goes in is the biasing policy (YAAT's own doc and VICE's code agree on this).
- **whisper.cpp grammar (GBNF)**: merged in 2023; non-matching tokens are scaled down by `grammar_penalty` rather than masked, and parse failures fall back to unconstrained sampling ([PR 1229](https://github.com/ggerganov/whisper.cpp/pull/1229)). Users report worse output from small grammar mistakes (a missing leading space) ([discussion 2003](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/discussions/2003)). Not exposed by Whisper.net or LM-Kit.
- **Transducer hotwords**: NeMo's GPU-PB phrase boosting works for CTC, RNN-T/TDT and Canary in greedy and beam decoding with 2-5 % speed cost and +8-10 points key-phrase F-score greedy, +17-23 beam ([TurboBias, arXiv 2508.07014](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07014); [NeMo word boosting docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/25.11/nemotoolkit/asr/asr_customization/word_boosting.html)), and per-utterance boosting lists landed for transducers ([NeMo PR 15125](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/pull/15125)). sherpa-onnx added modified beam search with per-stream hotwords for NeMo transducers including Parakeet-TDT (PR 3077, merged 2026-02-05) ([PR](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/3077); [hotwords docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/hotwords/index.html)); it needs a `bpe.vocab` exported from the NeMo tokenizer (the exporter now writes one). The C API has `SherpaOnnxCreateOfflineStreamWithHotwords`; the C# `OfflineRecognizer` wrapper exposes only `CreateStream()` and a config-level `HotwordsFile`, so per-PTT hotwords from .NET need one P/Invoke ([`c-api.h`](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/blob/master/sherpa-onnx/c-api/c-api.h)).
- **Phonetic entity resolution after ASR**: retrieve candidate entities by phonetic similarity (Double Metaphone, articulatory features) from a short context list, then replace or let an LLM decide; up to 30 % relative WER reduction on named entities when the list is filtered to similar-sounding candidates ([arXiv 2506.10779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10779)); denoising the candidate list before correction gave 28 % relative WER reduction ([DeRAGEC, ACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.786.pdf)); N-best plus a phonetic hint reduces LLM over-correction ([Yamashita et al., Interspeech 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/yamashita25_interspeech.html)).

## Findings: architecture options

- **Pipeline vs end-to-end.** The ATC literature and the one working trainer (VICE) use ASR → context-aware NLU. DLR's ontology-based extraction reached 96 % command recognition at ~3 % WER and stayed above 75 % at WER over 10 % ([Helmke et al., SIDs 2021](https://www.sesarju.eu/sites/default/files/documents/sid/2021/papers/SIDs_2021_paper_2.pdf)). Pipelines are testable stage by stage, which end-to-end models are not.
- **LLM structured extraction.** On a NARSIM simulator corpus, Llama 3.3 70B reached micro-F1 0.95 on human transcripts and 0.86 on fine-tuned Whisper output; a fine-tuned Gemma 3 4B improved notably on ~350 labels; transcription quality was the dominant factor, and over-prediction and hallucination needed verification steps ([SIDs 2025 paper 50](https://www.sesarju.eu/sites/default/files/documents/sid/2025/papers/SIDs_2025_paper_50-final.pdf)). YAAT already grammar-constrains its LLM fallback, which removes malformed output but not wrong-but-valid output.
- **Audio-LLMs with a grammar.** llama.cpp can feed audio to Gemma 4 E4B, Voxtral Mini 3B or Qwen3-ASR and constrain the generated text with GBNF, so one model could emit a canonical command (or a constrained transcript) straight from audio. No ATC result for this setup was found. Hotword-style prompting of LLM-decoder ASR is reported as unstable ([Qwen3-ASR#186](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/issues/186)).
- **Determinism.** whisper.cpp's default `temperature_inc` re-decodes at higher temperatures when entropy or log-probability thresholds trip; the maintainer recommends `--no-fallback` for deterministic output ([issue 734](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/issues/734); [commit f19e23f](https://github.com/ggerganov/whisper.cpp/commit/f19e23fbd108ec3ac458c7a19b31c930719e7a94)). Vulkan backends have produced wrong transcripts on some GPUs ([issue 2400](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/issues/2400)). Transducer greedy or fixed-width beam decoding has no sampling step. Voxtral's card says to always use temperature 0.
- **Confidence and rejection.** DLR scores a NO_CONCEPT/NO_CALLSIGN output as a rejection, not an error, which is the metric shape a "say again" design needs (next section). NeMo exposes word confidence; whisper.cpp exposes token probabilities (through Whisper.net, not LM-Kit).

## Findings: evaluation

- **Command-level metrics** (DLR, SESAR PJ.16-04 ontology): each command is one "word"; Levenshtein over the gold and extracted command lists gives Command Recognition Rate (matches / gold), Command Error Rate ((subs + ins) / gold) and Rejection Rate (deletions, including NO_CONCEPT / NO_CALLSIGN) / gold, plus the same three for callsigns alone ([Helmke et al., Interspeech 2021](https://elib.dlr.de/145465/1/Helmke_etal_INTERSPEECH2021_1-02-KeinWz.pdf); [SIDs 2021](https://www.sesarju.eu/sites/default/files/documents/sid/2021/papers/SIDs_2021_paper_2.pdf); ontology description updated 2025-07-04 at [elib.dlr.de/214852](https://elib.dlr.de/214852/)). A wrong command is worse than a rejected one, and the split makes that visible; YAAT's PASS/FAIL per case hides it.
- **Entity metrics**: callsign WER / callsign accuracy ([Zuluaga-Gomez et al., Interspeech 2021](https://publications.idiap.ch/attachments/papers/2021/Juan_INTERSPEECH2021_2021.pdf)); the same idea extends to fix, runway, taxiway and SID slots.
- **Unlabelled signal**: DLR's unclassified-word rate (share of words the extractor could not attach to a concept) flags likely ASR errors on untranscribed telemetry ([SIDs 2021](https://www.sesarju.eu/sites/default/files/documents/sid/2021/papers/SIDs_2021_paper_2.pdf)).
- **Lab vs field**: DLR found lab results can differ widely from ops-room results; synthetic TTS audio bounds phonetic coverage only, as YAAT's own baseline note says.
- **Run-to-run noise**: fix the decoding first (no temperature fallback, fixed beam, fixed thread count), then report the spread over N trials. The Open ASR Leaderboard moved its evaluation onto fixed hardware images for the same reason ([open_asr_leaderboard README](https://github.com/huggingface/open_asr_leaderboard)).

## Case study: VICE

VICE ([github.com/mmp/vice](https://github.com/mmp/vice), GPL-3.0-only, Go, TRACON-focused, master at [271a74d](https://github.com/mmp/vice/tree/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7) on 2026-10-04) added speech-to-text in its 2026 releases. All file links below are at that commit.

**Model and runtime.**

- whisper.cpp from a fork (`mmp/whisper.cpp`, branch `stt`) as a git submodule, wrapped in Go with cgo ([`.gitmodules`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/.gitmodules); [`autowhisper/`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/tree/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/autowhisper)). GPU through Vulkan on Windows and Linux, Metal on macOS; the Windows CI job installs the Vulkan SDK ([`.github/workflows/ci.yml`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/.github/workflows/ci.yml)). A GPU-init failure disables the GPU and falls back to CPU.
- Models: three ATC fine-tuned English Whisper models, all q5_0: `ggml-base.en-jlvatc-q5_0.bin` (55 MB), `ggml-small.en-jlvatc-q5_0.bin` (175 MB), `ggml-medium.en-jlvatc-q5_0.bin` (539 MB) ([`resources/models/manifest.json`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/resources/models/manifest.json)), downloaded as resources (moved from LFS to cloud storage in [7705c2e](https://github.com/mmp/vice/commit/7705c2e98)). "jlvatc" matches the naming of van Doorn's Whisper-ATC models (`jlvdoorn/whisper-*.en-atco2-asr`, `*-atcosim`); the repo does not state the source. The generic models were removed when the ATC tiers landed ([0b9d14d](https://github.com/mmp/vice/commit/0b9d14ddb), 2026-01-28).
- Model choice by **startup benchmark**: without a GPU it takes the base model; with one it benchmarks base → small → medium on 1 s of audio and keeps the largest that stays under 450 ms (continuing while under 300 ms), caching the choice per GPU device ([`client/stt.go` L659-L662, L846-L847](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/client/stt.go#L659-L662); [68259ef](https://github.com/mmp/vice/commit/68259efb9)). Benchmark results are reported to VICE's server.
- Decoding: **temperature fallback disabled** (`SetTemperatureFallback(-1.0)`) and **beam size 3** only when the benchmark measured faster than 20× real time ([`autowhisper/streaming.go` L116-L126](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/autowhisper/streaming.go#L116-L126); also [`autowhisper/transcribe.go` L189-L190](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/autowhisper/transcribe.go#L189-L190)).

**Biasing.** `makeWhisperPrompt` builds the prompt per PTT press from a state snapshot, assembled most-important-first and then reversed so the important items survive whisper.cpp's 223-token tail cap ([`client/stt.go` L1001-L1265](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/client/stt.go#L1001-L1265)). Priority order: fix telephony for the on-frequency aircraft (arrival airport plus up to five route fixes each, interleaved rank by rank across aircraft, plus the assigned approach's entry fixes), visual-approach reporting points, spoken callsigns (with type + last-three variants for N-numbers), approach types and runways, active approaches' fixes, SIDs and STARs in use, ATIS letters, and last a list of common phrases led by "niner". The comment's rationale: fix names are invented words Whisper cannot spell unprimed, while a garbled airline name is still recoverable by the callsign matcher. Fix telephony comes from `resources/sayfix.json`, a 70,952-entry fix-to-pronunciation map (e.g. `CEEGL` → "Seagull") maintained by contributors ([#1000](https://github.com/mmp/vice/pull/1000), 2026-09-20). Open issue [#999](https://github.com/mmp/vice/issues/999) notes the prompt spends many tokens on GA callsign variants.

**Transcript to command.** History: an OpenAI and then Anthropic API parser ([bf359ee](https://github.com/mmp/vice/commit/bf359ee3f), 2025-12-31) was replaced by a local algorithmic parser two weeks later ([87fc292](https://github.com/mmp/vice/commit/87fc29209), [d1e5b43](https://github.com/mmp/vice/commit/d1e5b438d), 2026-01-15). The current design ([`speech/stt/`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/tree/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/speech/stt)):

- Normalisation of known Whisper errors and numbers, then tokenisation ([`normalize.go`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/speech/stt/normalize.go)).
- One similarity primitive, `WordScore`, combining exact match, Double Metaphone identity or partial agreement, and Jaro-Winkler over metaphone codes, with every threshold declared in one file ([`score.go`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/speech/stt/score.go)).
- Callsign candidates matched against the aircraft on frequency; when two candidates are within 0.10 confidence, the one whose remainder parses as commands wins ([`callsign.go`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/speech/stt/callsign.go); `callsignJointMargin`, `score.go` L62).
- Scored template matching (per-template beam of 4) and a beam search (width 4) over segmentations of the post-callsign tokens into commands, discourse markers and skipped noise, ranked by coverage-adjusted log score ([`match.go`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/speech/stt/match.go); [`beam.go`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/speech/stt/beam.go)).
- Fixes matched by scoring every candidate fix of that aircraft against every 1-3 token span with one function; a spelled NATO correction ("that's delta papa kilo") overrides ([`fix.go`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/speech/stt/fix.go)).
- Validation against the aircraft's state, then the output contract: `CALLSIGN CMDS`, `CALLSIGN AGAIN` when the callsign is confident (≥ 0.93) but the commands are not, partial `SAYAGAIN/HEADING`-style requests for one unparsed slot alongside executed commands, `CORRECTION` / `ROLLBACK`, or nothing ([`provider.go` L33-L50, L282-L348](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/speech/stt/provider.go#L33-L50)).

**PTT, latency and execution.** PTT key `;` by default, audio streamed into the transcriber while held, pilot playback held during recording ([`client/stt.go` L1297-L1498](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/client/stt.go#L1297-L1498)). Decoded commands are **executed immediately** and the pilot reads back by TTS (Kokoro via sherpa-onnx since [d32f994](https://github.com/mmp/vice/commit/d32f99465)); an empty result after ≥ 2 s of audio makes the pilot ask for a retransmission. One logged case shows 396 ms Whisper time for a 2.4 s clip on an RTX 4060 with the medium model ([`speech/stt/tests/agn_one_fifty_seven_contact_tower.json`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/speech/stt/tests/agn_one_fifty_seven_contact_tower.json)). The user docs say STT expects FAA phraseology (7110.65 §2-4) and works better with a discrete GPU ([pharr.org/vice](https://pharr.org/vice/)).

**Evaluation.** Every transmission is logged to VICE's server with transcript, prompt, model, processor, timings and the aircraft context. `cmd/sttreview` and `cmd/stteval` triage those logs, keep entries that add **statement coverage** over the parser, and stage them as text-level regression cases after human sign-off ([`cmd/stteval/main.go`](https://github.com/mmp/vice/blob/271a74d3e41d0f91488ff20eedc7b80f169856d7/cmd/stteval/main.go)). The corpus holds 1,222 accepted cases in `speech/stt/tests/` and 37 known failures in `speech/stt/failing_tests/`. No aggregate accuracy figure is published in the repo, docs or issues found; open STT issues are feature gaps (CTAF [#934](https://github.com/mmp/vice/issues/934), "unable" for VFR [#978](https://github.com/mmp/vice/issues/978)) and the prompt-token budget [#999](https://github.com/mmp/vice/issues/999).

**What YAAT can adopt.** VICE's code and `sayfix.json` are GPL-3.0; YAAT is MIT, so nothing can be copied without relicensing. What transfers as design:

- Temperature fallback off and a fixed beam: needs a runtime that exposes them (Whisper.net or whisper.cpp directly), which LM-Kit does not.
- Per-PTT, tail-priority prompt from on-frequency aircraft (fixes first). YAAT abandoned per-PTT prompts under an earlier model; VICE's evidence is anecdotal, so measure it.
- A fix pronunciation dictionary as an input to both the prompt and the matcher. YAAT would need its own source (FAA fix names have no official pronunciation; VICE's is crowd-maintained), or permission from VICE's maintainer.
- A single scored similarity function, joint callsign/command decoding, beam over segmentations, state validation and a three-way output (command / say again / nothing). This is the closest working analogue to the NLU YAAT needs, and it is local and deterministic.
- Telemetry → coverage-selected text regression corpus: YAAT has the telemetry and `promote` already; coverage-based selection is the missing piece.

VICE's limits for YAAT: TRACON scope only (no taxi routes, pushback, ground phraseology), Whisper-only, auto-execution, and no published accuracy numbers.

## Candidate architectures

All three share one new **text-to-command stage** (below) and one **evaluation** (command-level metrics). They differ in the acoustic model.

Shared text-to-command stage: per-PTT `SpeechContext` (on-frequency callsigns with spoken forms, each aircraft's fixes/route/SID/STAR/approach, airport runways, taxiways, parking spots) → normalisation → callsign candidates scored against on-frequency aircraft → scored template matching over YAAT's rule catalogue with a beam over segmentations → slot resolution of fixes/SIDs/taxiways/runways against the aircraft's own lists with one phonetic similarity function → state validation → output: canonical command with a confidence, a "say again" result, or nothing. The rule catalogue (`PhraseologyRules`) stays the source of templates and of pilot readbacks. The LLM fallback becomes optional, scored against the same metrics.

### A. Whisper-ATC, decoding under our control, dynamic prompt

- **What changes**: replace LM-Kit `SpeechToText` with Whisper.net (or a thin whisper.cpp binding) to set temperature 0, no fallback, fixed beam, fixed threads, and read token probabilities; build the prompt per PTT, VICE-style. Model: jacktol Whisper-medium ATC (current) or the larger jacktol large-v3 ATC fine-tune, A/B'd.
- **Expected gains**: run-to-run variance should collapse if fallback is its cause (to be shown by the harness); fix and SID mishears should drop where the prompt now primes them; token probabilities feed confidence and rejection.
- **Costs**: model 0.5-1.5 GB (unchanged); warm GPU latency ~230 ms for the medium ATC model and ~370 ms for large-v3-turbo, measured on an RTX 4090 (pipeline doc); CPU feasible only with smaller models. Whisper.net is MIT, whisper.cpp MIT. Integration: a new `ISttEngine` behind `SpeechRecognitionService`; LM-Kit stays for the LLM unless dropped. Small to medium effort.
- **Risks**: the 223-token cap limits context on busy frequencies; Whisper on 1-2 word clips hallucinates; jacktol models train on NC-SA data.

### B. Parakeet-TDT ATC fine-tune with per-PTT hotwords (sherpa-onnx)

- **What changes**: Parakeet-TDT 0.6B (v2 English or v3) fine-tuned on ATC data plus synthetic FAA-phraseology audio from YAAT's own template generator and Piper voices, exported to sherpa-onnx int8 with `bpe.vocab`; per-PTT hotwords (fix telephony, callsigns, SIDs) through modified beam search; same NLU.
- **Expected gains**: hotword boosting attacks the largest failure cluster directly, without a token cap; decoding has no sampling, so it should be deterministic at fixed threads; the public ATC fine-tune reports a slightly better WER than Whisper-large-v3 ATC on the same split at under half the parameters; CPU int8 removes the GPU requirement.
- **Costs**: ~0.6B params, int8 export a few hundred MB (size of YAAT's export unmeasured); CPU latency unmeasured (the sandbox's `--parakeet` path measures it). CC-BY-4.0 base model. Integration: bump sherpa-onnx 1.12.40 → 1.13.x, one P/Invoke for `CreateOfflineStreamWithHotwords`, promote `SherpaSttEngine` out of the sandbox. A NeMo fine-tuning pipeline (Python, one CUDA GPU, offline) is new work. Medium to large effort.
- **Risks**: stock Parakeet failed YAAT's two real clips; without a fine-tune this option has no evidence. Hotword over-boosting causes false insertions (TurboBias lowered the weight for that reason). Training data licence (UWB-ATCC NC-SA). Lower-case, no ATC numerals: normalisation must be redone for its output style.

### C. Audio-LLM with grammar-constrained output

- **What changes**: send the PTT audio to an audio-capable LLM (Gemma 4 E4B, already shipped as the text fallback; or Qwen3-ASR / Voxtral Mini) through llama.cpp with a GBNF grammar built per PTT from `CanonicalCommandGrammar` plus the scenario's callsign and fix lists, emitting either the canonical command or a constrained transcript.
- **Expected gains**: one pass joins acoustics and command semantics; the grammar makes impossible entities unproduceable.
- **Costs**: E4B ~3.5 GB VRAM quantised (LM-Kit's own guide figure), latency for a 4B model on audio unmeasured; LM-Kit does not document LLM audio input, so this needs llama.cpp directly (sidecar `llama-server` or a binding). Large effort.
- **Risks**: no ATC evidence; LLM-decoder ASR hallucinates on silence and with hotword prompts; a grammar forces *some* valid command out of noise unless rejection is designed in; hardest of the three to test stage by stage.

## Recommendation

1. **Evaluation first.** Add command-level scoring (recognition / error / rejection rate, callsign and fix accuracy) to `--eval` and `--atc-ouroboros`, keep PASS/FAIL as a summary, and make wrong commands count worse than rejections. Add radio-band filtering and noise to the synthetic audio, and more than one voice pack, so synthetic numbers stop flattering every model. This is the gate for every later choice.
2. **Determinism next.** Move Whisper off LM-Kit to a runtime with temperature fallback off and a fixed beam (architecture A's runtime change) and confirm with the harness whether the 104/200 variance disappears. If it does, YAAT-225 is closed by this step.
3. **Rebuild the text-to-command stage** as described above, taking VICE's design ideas (scored similarity, joint callsign/command decoding, beam over segmentations, state validation, say-again output) and YAAT's own rule catalogue. Feed it the per-PTT context, including each aircraft's fixes and SIDs, which the current pipeline never passes to Whisper.
4. **Bake-off, then choose the acoustic model**: A (Whisper-ATC, dynamic prompt) against B (Parakeet-TDT ATC fine-tune, per-PTT hotwords), same NLU, same harness, real telemetry cases as the deciding set. Choose B only if it wins on command error rate at an acceptable CPU latency; otherwise keep A.
5. **C as a time-boxed spike** after step 3, because it shares the context and grammar work and may simplify the fallback, but it should not be the base.

VICE weighed against these: VICE is architecture A with step 3 done, on Vulkan, minus the evaluation rigour of step 1. YAAT can reach VICE's design with A plus the new NLU; B is the step beyond VICE, aimed at the entity errors that prompts alone do not fix.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Hardware floor.** Must speech work on CPU-only or integrated-GPU machines? That decides between Whisper-medium/large on GPU and Parakeet int8 on CPU, and whether a VICE-style startup benchmark is needed.
2. **Training-data licence.** The current default model and every public ATC fine-tune trained on UWB-ATCC (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) while declaring MIT. Is shipping such a model acceptable for YAAT (free, MIT), or must a fine-tune avoid NC data (ATCO2 and ATCOSIM terms to be checked, plus YAAT's own synthetic and consented telemetry audio)?
3. **LM-Kit.** Keep it only for the LLM, drop it entirely, or keep Whisper on it? Its STT API hides the decoding controls this plan needs.
4. **Fine-tuning appetite.** Is an offline Python/NeMo (or HF) fine-tuning pipeline, run on one CUDA GPU, acceptable as a maintained part of the repo?
5. **Telemetry as training data.** Speech telemetry is collected under an opt-in that promises samples are not published. May they be used to fine-tune a model that is published, or only to evaluate?
6. **Auto-send.** Commands always land in the input box for review today. Should solo training execute recognised commands directly, with the pilot saying "say again" on rejection as VICE does, while RPO/instructor mode keeps the review step?
7. **VICE.** Contact VICE's maintainer about licensing `sayfix.json` (or sharing a pronunciation dataset) and about a shared ATC-STT test corpus? Their code is GPL-3.0.
8. **Latency budget.** What PTT-release-to-command latency is acceptable (VICE accepts ≤ 450 ms for 1 s of audio)?
9. **Scope order.** Tower/ground (taxi routes, taxiway letters, pushback) and TRACON (fixes, SIDs, approaches) stress different entities; which should the first bake-off weight?
