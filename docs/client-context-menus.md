# Aircraft context menus — design

The aircraft right-click menus on the radar, ground and aircraft-list views. This doc holds the settled design. The step list and the reviewed default situation table are in [plans/context-menu-quick-commands.md](plans/context-menu-quick-commands.md); the mocks are the repo copies in `plans/canvases/quick-command-icons/` (the icon strip) and `plans/canvases/ground-context-menu-mocks/` (the ground point menu).

## The menu

- **Quick commands by situation.** An aircraft's menu shows a short flat list of quick commands chosen by its situation, with the full tree one level down under **All Commands**. Situations, not raw phases: about 20 named situations, each a set of phases plus DTO flags. Both terms are in [`CONTEXT.md`](../CONTEXT.md).
- **Top level:** the header (the bold title `{callsign} · {type}`, or the bare callsign without a type, the state line, the route summary and hold status rows), a separator, the **For section** while another aircraft is selected, the icon strip and the text quick commands, a separator, `Command…`, `Note…`, All Commands ▸, Favorite Commands ▸, a separator, then Delete and the RPO items.

  The view section (the radar's Display ▸ and Draw route, the ground's Display ▸, nothing on the aircraft list) sits last in All Commands after a separator, not at the root (`AircraftMenuBuilder.AddViewSection`). Track, Data block and Squawk sit under All Commands › Track and coordination.

  A delayed spawn keeps the header, then `Command…` and `Note…` between separators (its release strip, when it has one, comes first). A surface live-traffic shadow keeps the header, then `Command…` and `Note…` between separators, Favorite Commands ▸ and All Commands ▸ holding only Track and coordination and then the view section after a separator (`AircraftMenuBuilder.AddSurfaceShadow`).

  The radar point menu keeps its view section at the root.

  Last comes **Settings for this view…** (added by each view's right-click handler, not the builder: `RadarView.BuildAircraftRightClickMenu`, `GroundView.BuildAircraftRightClickMenu`, `DataGridView.BuildRowContextMenu`).
- **Track** (All Commands › Track and coordination › Track) lists only what the *track's own state* calls for, and the same items on every view: an untracked aircraft gets **Initiate Track**; an owned track gets **Initiate handoff…**, **Point out…** and **Drop track**; a handoff in progress gets **Accept handoff** and **Cancel handoff**; a pending pointout gets **Acknowledge pointout**. States that overlap show the union, each group separated (an owned track with a handoff under way shows the three owned items, a rule, then Accept and Cancel). The filter reads the track alone — the instructor acts as whichever position owns or receives it, so no "my position" test enters it. Headers, predicates and the group order are `SharedMenuGroups.Track` / `TrackGroups` and the `track.*` `MenuCatalog` entries.
- **For section** (`SharedMenuGroups.AddForSection`) opens the menu under the header while another aircraft that has spawned is selected and a relative pair applies: the bold label `For {selected} (selected)`, for an airborne pair the dimmed line `{clicked} is at its {n} o'clock, {d} nm, {altitude}` (`RelativeGeometry.Describe`; the clock from the selected aircraft's true heading, `same altitude` under 100 ft), then Report in sight and Follow; for a ground pair the dimmed line `{clicked} is {state} {place}, {feet} ft {ahead|behind}` with ` on {selected}'s route` when `IsOnTaxiRoute` (`RelativeGeometry.DescribeGround`), then Follow and Give way to. The items are plain menu items with their quick-command glyph, sent as the selected aircraft. A separator and the bold `For {clicked}` label then head the clicked aircraft's own items (the strip and text quick commands). A mixed pair gets no section.
- **All Commands** holds the full tree grouped by task, in one fixed order (`AircraftMenuBuilder.AddAllCommands`; a group with nothing in it is left out). A live-traffic shadow that can be assumed starts with its Assume items and a separator. Then come the seven task categories (`MenuCategory`, labels from `MenuCategories.Label`; Live traffic and Sim are categories too, but their items have no group header here): **Runway** (the tower's clearances), **Taxi** (the ground-movement block, Taxi to runway ▸ and Preset taxi route ▸ among it, then Draw taxi route…), **Vectors**, **Approach**, **Pattern**, **Pilot reports** and **Track and coordination**. A separator then precedes the sim-control items: Warp… (airborne controllable aircraft only, `AircraftCommandApplicability.IsAirborneControllable`) and Release to live feed.

  The view section (Display ▸, and Draw route on the radar) comes last after one more separator.

  Vectors has four blocks under bold labels (`SharedMenuGroups.Vectors`): Heading… (Present heading, Fly heading and the turns), Altitude…, Speed… and Route (headed `Route (→ FIX)` while the aircraft navigates to a fix): Direct to, Append direct to, Proceed on course, Cross fix, Depart fix, Join airway, Join radial outbound / inbound and Climb via SID. Approach has three blocks: the approach pickers and Cleared visual; Join STAR, Descend via STAR and PTAC; the holds. Pattern holds the pattern entries and maneuvers, Make left / right closed traffic and Follow traffic…. Pilot reports holds Report field in sight and Report traffic in sight, then Report when…, then Ask pilot to say…. Track and coordination has the bold-labelled blocks Track, Data block, Squawk and Coordination, then after a separator Edit flight plan.

  `MenuCategories.Of(id)` gives a catalog id's task category (the All Commands group it sits in); the ids in no group (Favorites, Command…, Note…, the spawn, point-menu and For-section items) are listed in `MenuCategories.Uncategorized`, and a test holds every `MenuCatalog` id to exactly one of the two.

  In a ground phase, on the ground with no phase, a takeoff still on the ground, a landing roll and the touch-and-go variants Vectors, Approach and Pattern are left out and Pilot reports keeps only Ask pilot to say… (`AircraftMenuBuilder.HidesFlightCommands`). Each entry shows by its own catalog predicate, never by the situation flags.
- **Flight rules are a runtime filter, not separate lists.** Each situation has one list; every entry carries *Both* / *IFR only* / *VFR only*, defaulted from the catalog item and overridable per entry, evaluated against `FlightRules` when the menu opens. An aircraft with no flight rules filed gets only the *Both* entries; a *VFR only* entry reaches an IFR aircraft under the "VFR commands for IFR aircraft" setting, as All Commands offers it (every one under *All*, straight-in final alone under *Enter final only*).
- **An entry is a catalog item or custom text.** Catalog items are stable IDs for the menu's items and submenus, keeping their pickers, smart runway defaults and applicability predicates; custom text is a command template with the same substitution as Favorites. A quick entry builds through its catalog entry's own builder, so it behaves exactly as the same item under All Commands.
- **Inapplicable entries are hidden, not disabled.** An issued clearance hides itself and shows its cancel entry instead.
- **Every command row shows its alias.** A row built from a catalog entry that sends a verb of its own (All Commands, the text quick commands, the point menu) shows the verb a user would type, right-aligned in the dimmed monospace command font, so a mouse user learns the typed command. `MenuAliases.CanonicalVerbs` lists, per catalog id, the verbs its row sends (any alias of each); `MenuAliases.NoVerb` lists the ids with none (Command…, Note…, Edit flight plan, Favorites, Draw taxi route…, Push route…), and a test holds every `MenuCatalog` id to exactly one of the two.

  `MenuAliases.Display(id, scheme)` maps each listed verb to the *primary verb* of its command type in the controller's `CommandScheme` (`CommandPattern.PrimaryVerb`, the pattern's first alias, as the command bar completes it), so the alias follows a customised scheme; a verb the registry or the scheme does not know shows as listed. One verb shows alone, two as `A / B`, three or more as `A …`. `MenuSession.Scheme` carries the scheme (`MenuContext.Scheme`; `ClientMenuHost` passes `Preferences.CommandScheme`).

  `MenuAlias.Apply` records the alias on the row's `MenuItem` (attached property `MenuAlias.Text`) and gives it `MenuAliasTemplate`, which draws the label and the alias in two columns so a submenu's verbs line up; the row's header stays its string label and its automation name stays the label. It runs for the text quick commands (`AircraftMenuBuilder`) and for the leaves the shared groups build (`SharedMenuGroups.TryLeaf`), and skips a row whose header is not a plain string or already has a template. Rows inside a flyout (`SharedMenuGroups.FlyoutRow`: the Squawk, Ask pilot and Clear Scratchpad rows) name their command another way and show no alias.

  A build that sends one of several verbs records its alias itself (`MenuCatalog.WithOwnAlias`, `MenuAliases.Display(IReadOnlyList<string>, CommandScheme)`), and `Apply` keeps it: Warp here shows `WARPG` at a taxi node and `WARP` at a map point.
- **The icon strip** shows every glyph-bearing entry of the one per-situation list, with no cap; every other entry is a text entry below it. A list left with no glyph-bearing entry has no strip.

  The icons are grouped by category (`MenuCategories.Of`; Live traffic, Runway, Taxi, Vectors, Approach, Pattern, Pilot reports, Track and coordination and Sim, entries in no category last), the categories in the order the list first names them and each group in list order, six to a row (`QuickCommandStripLayout.Pack`, shared by the menu and the Settings preview). Each group is a segment under a small upper-case label (RUNWAY, TAXI, ..., TRACK AND COORDINATION, LIVE TRAFFIC, SIM) with a tick and a trailing rule; a label too long for its segment ends in "…". Neighbouring groups share a row when they fit in six, a thin vertical rule between them (in a row where any segment is labelled, every segment keeps a label line's height, so the cells align).

  A category of more than six wraps: its later rows are unlabelled continuations, and its last partial row may be shared with the groups after it, its own part first. Entries in no category (custom entries) form one last unlabelled group.

  **Icons off.** The setting *Show quick command icons* (`UserPreferences.ShowQuickCommandIcons`, default on; `MenuSession.ShowQuickCommandIcons`, `MenuContext.ShowQuickCommandIcons`) drops every strip.

  The aircraft menu then lists the quick commands as text rows with their aliases under bold category labels in `MenuCategory` order (Live traffic, Runway, Taxi, Vectors, Approach, Pattern, Pilot reports, Track and coordination, Sim), the release items among Track and coordination's, then the entries in no category (custom entries) unlabelled after a separator (`AircraftMenuBuilder.AddQuickCommandRows`); then `Command…` onward as usual.

  A delayed spawn's release items become text rows after a separator. A live-traffic shadow's Assume items and the Warp and Delete items list under Live traffic and Sim labels likewise.

  The strip's category labels apply to the aircraft menu only: the point menu's strip and a delayed spawn's release strip are one flat row with no labels (`QuickCommandStrip.FlatFromBuilt`). With icons off, the radar point menu lists Fly heading (with its turn row), Direct to, Append direct to, Hold left, Hold right, a separator and Warp here as text rows (`AddRadarPointRows`).

  The release items that apply (Release (HFR), an open padlock glyph, then Check release window, a clock) sit in Track and coordination at their place in the situation's list, or after the list's icons when the list does not name them (`AircraftMenuBuilder.WithRelease`). A live-traffic shadow's Assume items (Assume, Assume and track, Assume selected, Unassume) form the Live traffic group, so its strip is one row under LIVE TRAFFIC; Warp and Delete form the Sim group. A delayed spawn held for release, or with a call-for-release window, shows just those icons as a strip under its header (`AddReleaseStrip`); with neither, its menu is unchanged.

  A button stands for its entry's own menu item: a sending or prompting item is clicked through, and a submenu (Cleared for takeoff ▸) opens as a flyout beside the menu, its top level with the icon strip (on the menu's left near the screen's right edge), marked by a corner notch; choosing a command closes the menu.

  A submenu whose items follow live data (Push back to…, filling from a seed and then a live plan; Follow… and Give way to…, refilled from the ground traffic when they open and once a second while open; Hold short of…, refilled from the route likewise; Preset taxi route, refilled from the host's presets likewise; Exit left / right, refilled likewise; Taxi to runway is the exception, asked once when it first opens) registers `QuickCommandStrip.FollowInFlyout` with an `IStripFlyoutContent`: the strip tells it when the flyout opens, with the flyout's own item list and an action that closes the flyout and the menu, when the pointer enters and leaves the flyout (`PointerInside`), and when it closes, so the flyout follows the list while open instead of showing a frozen copy.

  The Air taxi and Land flyouts register the same way and refill and re-sort likewise.

  The icon is latched (style class `sticky`, a light background) while its flyout is open; clicking it again, or Enter or Space on it, toggles the flyout, and Escape closes it and leaves the menu open. The flyout stays open when the pointer leaves the menu or rests on a plain row, and closes when another icon opens its flyout, when another row opens its own submenu or when the menu closes. `QuickCommandStrip.OpenSubmenu(Button)` opens it as a click does.

  The strip has no label row or tooltip: the entry under the pointer (or keyboard focus) is named in the header's state line (`QuickCommandLabelLine`, the dimmed row under the title), in place of its text for as long as it is pointed at. The label is the entry's name, then the command it sends when it sends one fixed command (`Hold position — HOLD`), and "›" when the entry opens a submenu. The line keeps its size, so the menu does not resize. A label wider than the line scrolls as a marquee (800 ms hold at each end, 60 px/s, back and repeat; `MarqueeClock` is the test clock the marquee reads time from, and `RenderMarqueeFrame` draws one frame); one that fits stays still. Marquee frames run on a UI-owned dispatcher timer that stops when the line detaches, so none is posted after the menu is gone (`MarqueeFramesRunning` tells a test whether the timer is running). An aircraft with no state line keeps a blank line of that height under its title for the label (`QuickCommandLabelLine.Ensure`).

  Glyphs are coloured by family (tower, ground, flight, pattern, scope and sim); a left/right pair shares one glyph, and so do Cleared straight-in and Join straight-in, and Cancel landing clearance and Cancel takeoff clearance, which no situation offers together. Each forced approach verb draws its base verb's glyph with the force mark (`QuickCommandGlyph.HasForceMark`: an amber disc with "!" at the lower right, drawn by `QuickCommandStrip.AddForceMark`). Join approach, the straight-in pair, Join final approach course and the force pair have glyphs, so any can be a strip button in a custom quick list. A pattern entry's strip icon turns to the aircraft's assigned runway (see below).
- **Cleared for takeoff flyout.** The strip's quick-command flyout and All Commands › Runway › Cleared for takeoff share one builder, `TakeoffFlyout` (`MenuCatalog` calls it). A VFR flyout opens with an "Initial altitude" box (placeholder "default climb"), pre-filled from the filed `VFR/NNN` (`IMenuAircraft.FiledVfrCruiseFeet`, shown as hundreds, `045`), then the rows: Cleared for takeoff (`CTO`), Make left / right closed traffic (`CTO MLT` / `CTO MRT`), Straight out (`CTO MSO [alt]`), Left / right crosswind departure (`MLC` / `MRC`), Left / right downwind departure (`MLD` / `MRD`), Left / right turnout (45°) (`ML45` / `MR45`), Fly heading [box] (`CTO {hdg} [alt]`), On course (`CTO OC [alt]`, disabled with no destination or a destination equal to the departure), Turn left / right direct [fix box] (`CTO TLDCT` / `TRDCT {fix} [alt]`), a separator and the Custom row (below).

  An IFR flyout is Cleared for takeoff, Fly heading [box] (`CTO {hdg}`, three digits, no altitude), Fly runway heading (`CTO RH`) and the Custom row; under "VFR commands for IFR aircraft: All" the IFR rows come first, then a separator, the altitude box and the VFR rows, and the Custom row stays last. Every row shows the command it sends and rewrites it as the box changes; a row with an empty or unreadable box shows "···", fades and sends nothing. Enter in a fix or heading box sends that row, and the fix box suggests fixes through `IMenuHost.SuggestFixes`. Glyphs are pattern purple, turned to the departure runway.

  The Custom row (`TakeoffFlyout.CustomItem`, `TakeoffRowInput.Custom`) follows a separator at the end of the flyout and is always open: a box with the fixed `CTO` drawn inside it (`TakeoffFlyout.CustomBoxName`, placeholder `CustomPlaceholder`). `TakeoffFlyout.CustomCommand` trims the text and sends it after `CTO`: blank sends `CTO`, `28R` sends `CTO 28R`, and spaces are kept (`CTO RH 3000`). A click on the row or Enter in the box sends.

  The Squawk, Scratchpad and Say flyouts hold the same kind of row, `InlineBoxRow` (`InlineBoxRow.Create(InlineBoxSpec, send)`, whose `Item` is its menu item), built from an `InlineBoxSpec` (label, glyph, verb drawn in the box, placeholder, box name and width, `Seed`, the text the box opens holding, and `Read`, which turns the text into an `InlineBoxReading`: `Sends` a command, `Refuses` with a reason, or `Blank`). The command column shows the command, the verb and `···` dimmed while blank, or `can't send` in red; a blank or refused box sends nothing, Enter or a click on a sendable row sends and closes, and otherwise the item stays open (`StaysOpenOnClick`).

  The refusal is drawn by `RefusableBox` (`FlyoutPanelParts.cs`): the box keeps its frame, a 2 px red bar lies over its bottom edge and a red reason line sits under it, shared with `FlyoutPanelParts.Refusal`, which the fix-value flyouts use for their lines.

  The Squawk flyout (`SharedMenuGroups.SquawkFlyout`, the Track and coordination group's Squawk block and the Squawk… entry alike) opens on the code box (`MenuCatalog.SquawkCodeRow`, verb `SQ`, box `SquawkCodeBox`), focused as it opens, from a submenu or from a quick-command strip button alike (`SharedMenuGroups.FocusTheBoxOnOpen`). Four octal digits send `SQ nnnn`; other text is refused with `InvalidSquawkCode` ("Not a valid squawk code (0000-7777)"); blank sends nothing, since bare `SQ` is Squawk assigned code.

  After a separator come Squawk assigned code (left out for an aircraft with no assigned code: `SquawkRow` returns null when the entry's `IsApplicable` fails), random, VFR, normal and standby, then after another separator Ident; every row leads with a glyph column, blank where the entry has no glyph.

  The Scratchpad flyout (`SharedMenuGroups.ScratchpadFlyout`, the Data block block's Scratchpad… entry) opens on the Scratchpad box (`MenuCatalog.ScratchpadRow`, verb `SP`, box `ScratchpadBox`, placeholder `up to {max} characters`), focused as it opens. The box opens holding the aircraft's present Scratchpad 1 (`IMenuAircraft.Scratchpad1`, never the destination the scope shows in its place), all selected, and selects it again each time it regains the focus while the text is still the seed (`InlineBoxSpec.Seed`).

  The text is sent trimmed and upper-cased after `SP`. A blank box, or the present value in any case, sends nothing, since a bare `SP` clears and `SP` with the present value toggles back to the previous scratchpad. Text longer than the room's limit (`IMenuHost.ScratchpadMaxLength`, `ScratchpadTooLong`) is refused inline. The limit is the student facility's, sent as `ScratchpadMaxLength` on the position display config and kept by `MainViewModel.ScratchpadMaxLength`; 0 or less (no scenario, no STARS config, an older checkpoint) reads as the default 3 (`ScratchpadRuleEngine.MaxScratchpadLength(null)`).

  After a separator come Clear Scratchpad 1 and Clear Scratchpad 2 (bare `SP1` and `SP2`), each shown only while its slot holds a value, since a bare clear on an empty slot restores the previous value; with neither shown, there is no separator.

  The Say flyout (`SharedMenuGroups.AskPilotFlyout`, All Commands' Ask pilot to say… and its Custom… entry alike) lists the six queries, a separator and the Custom box (`MenuCatalog.SayCustomRow`, verb `SAY`, box `SayCustomBox`), focused as it opens, from a submenu or a strip button; blank sends nothing. The text popup (`IMenuHost.ShowInputPopup`: placeholder, seeded text, caret and callback, trimmed, a blank submit closes) is left to Custom taxi on the point menu, the one menu item that still opens it.

  A click on a box row or a box itself must not close the menu: `QuickCommandStrip.CloseOnChoice` skips a click already handled and an item marked `StaysOpenOnClick`.

  The Warp flyout (`WarpFlyout.Build`, in `WarpFlyout.cs`) is the one builder of Warp… (All Commands, `MenuCatalog.BuildWarp`, an empty FRD box) and of Warp here at a map point (`BuildPointWarp`, the box seeded with the clicked point's FRD by `WarpFrdSeed.ClickedPoint`, labelled `Fix or FRD · the clicked point`).

  It opens with a disabled title line (`Warp`, then `{callsign} · now {hdg}° · {alt} ft · {spd} kt`, the heading magnetic).

  Four rows follow, built by `InlineBoxRow.CreateSendingAll`: Fix or FRD (an `AutoCompleteBox` fed by `IMenuHost.SuggestFixes`, led by the Warp glyph, no verb inside the box, focused as it opens), Heading, Altitude (hundreds, feet or FL) and Speed, whose glyph cells are empty (`InlineBoxSpec.BlankGlyphSlot`), then a separator and the send line (`WarpSendLine`) showing the whole command at full opacity.

  Each row's command column shows what its box puts in the command, and its box reads through `FixList.TypedFixOrFrd`, `FixValueFlyout.ParseHeading` (001-360), `MenuCatalog.ParseAltitudeFeet` (so `110`, `11000` and `FL110` agree; refused as `AltitudeFlyout.InvalidAltitude`, and under 100 ft too) and a positive whole-knot speed (`SpeedFlyout.InvalidSpeed`). Typed values get a green line under the box (`heading 090 magnetic`, `→ 80,000 ft`, `250 kt indicated`); blanks get an amber one.

  The Fix or FRD box's green line reads an FRD back as `{d} NM on the {r}° radial of {fix} (magnetic)` (`10 NM on the 090° radial of OAK (magnetic)`) and a plain fix as `{fix} · fix` (`SFO · fix`). A no-digit entry that is not a known fix waits, with no refusal and its column `···`, for as long as `IMenuHost.SuggestFixes` still offers names for it; the refusal shows once nothing is suggested.

  A field row whose box is refused shows `✕` in its command column (`InlineBoxRow.RefusedMark`, only on `CreateSendingAll` rows), since the send line says `can't send`; rows built by `InlineBoxRow.Create` (Squawk, Scratchpad, Say Custom, CTO Custom) keep `can't send` (`InlineBoxRow.CantSend`) in their own column.

  The command is `WARP {frd}` then each field up to the last filled one. The sim reads the fields left to right, so a blank before a filled one sends the present value in its place (`InlineBoxReading.Keeps`, the command column amber, `kept: … sent in its place, since a gap would shift the slots`): the heading rounded magnetic (0 reads 360), the altitude as hundreds of the present altitude rounded to the nearest 100 ft, the speed rounded IAS.

  A trailing blank is left out (`InlineBoxReading.LeavesOut`, the column `—`, `left out: the sim keeps …`). When the present altitude rounds below 100 ft there is no altitude word `AltitudeResolver` reads as it, so a blank Altitude before a filled Speed refuses (`Blank before a filled field, and the present {n} ft has no word the sim reads as it: type an altitude`), never `WARP … 0`; an 800 ft aircraft with only the speed typed sends the altitude as `8`.

  Only Enter in a box (`InlineBoxRow.SendsCommand`) and a click on the send line send, and both close the menu; a click on a field row focuses its box and keeps the menu open. A blank FRD box waits with no refusal under it and the send line reads `can't send` in red, as it does for any refused field. Row boxes and send line follow each other through `InlineBoxRow.RefollowSend`, since one field's text changes what another reads as.

  Warp is offered only to an airborne controllable aircraft, because WARP leaves the aircraft airborne: the `Warp…` entry's predicate is `IsAirborneControllable`, and Warp here at a map point needs the same (`MenuCatalog.CanWarpHere`). At a taxi node (`point.Node ?? point.WarpNode`) the one `PointWarpHere` entry is the ground warp `WARPG #{id}` for any controllable aircraft; it opens no flyout.
- **Fly heading flyout.** The strip's Fly heading button and All Commands › Vectors › Heading › Fly heading, Turn left, Turn right, Turn left (degrees) and Turn right (degrees) share one builder, `HeadingFlyout.Build(HeadingFlyoutPreset, ...)` (`MenuCatalog.HeadingEntry`), registered through `QuickCommandStrip.FollowInFlyout`. The 72-row heading list popup and the relative-turn list popup are gone. A `HeadingFlyoutPreset` is the entry's label, the toggle's `HeadingVerb` (`FlyHeading` `FH`, `TurnLeft` `TL`, `TurnRight` `TR`) and the `HeadingFlyoutFocus` it opens on: Turn left / right open with the toggle on that verb and focus in the box, the two degree entries on Fly heading with focus on their side's 30° chip. Present heading stays a plain `FPH` leaf.

  `HeadingFlyoutPanel` holds, top to bottom, the title `Fly heading · now 270`, the Present heading row (`FPH`, `Present heading · 270 now`), the verb toggle, the Heading box (`HeadingFlyout.BoxPlaceholder`, showing its command), the north-up `HeadingRose` between the `← Left by` and `Right by →` chip columns (`HeadingFlyout.DegreeTurns`: 5° 10° 15° 20° 30° 45° 60° 90°, sending `LT n` / `RT n`) and a readout (`HeadingFlyout.ReadoutHint`, then e.g. `300 · a right turn of 30° · FH 300` from `TurnText`). The rose has a dead zone of `DeadZoneRadius` at its centre.

  A rose click or a drag's release, and Enter in the box, send the toggle's verb with a three-digit heading (`HeadingFlyout.Command`: `FH 005`, `FH 360`); `BoxCommand` also reads `L180` as `TL 180` and `R090` as `TR 090`. A drag released more than 30 px outside the rim sends nothing. Focus lands in the box; Down or Tab focuses the rose at the present heading rounded to five, ←/↓ and →/↑ step `Step` (5°) or `ShiftStep` (30°) with Shift, Enter sends, Tab goes on to the chips, Left and Right cross the chip columns, Escape closes.

  The flyout is live: once a second on `FlyoutLiveTick` the needle, the "now" heading, the Present heading row, the chip tooltips (`LT 20 → 250`) and the readout follow the aircraft, while the picked heading stays. Tests: `tests/Yaat.Client.UI.Tests/Views/HeadingFlyoutTests.cs`; the showcase shot is the GuideCapture scene `whats-new-heading-flyout`.
- **Pattern-entry runway flyout.** Enter left / right downwind, Enter left / right base and Make straight-in always open a runway flyout, from All Commands and from the quick-command strip: a submenu over every runway end at the aircraft's destination (else departure) airport, with the focus on the assigned runway's row when there is one. With no runway data there, an assigned runway makes the entry a leaf that sends the verb for it, and with neither the entry is disabled with the reason beside its label.

  `MenuCatalog.BuildPatternRunwayFlyout` builds one row per end from `IMenuHost.GetPatternRunwayChoices` (the host builds them with `PatternRunwayChoices.For` once per airport and physical type per menu build): the room's active arrival or both-way ends first (a departure-only end is not active here), a separator, then the rest. Within each group the ends the aircraft can land on come first and the short ones last, each part in runway order. With no active runway, the order is runway order with short ends last.

  A row is a `MenuGlyphRow`: the entry's glyph, "Runway 28R", and the command (`ELD 28R`). The glyph is the lands-west glyph rotated clockwise by the end's true course less 270° (`PatternRunwayChoices.Glyph`, `QuickCommandGlyph.RotationDegrees`), so the arrowhead points the landing direction.

  Make straight-in is mirrored top to bottom when the end's default pattern side is right (`PatternGeometry.InferDefaultPatternDirection`: an L/R parallel's outboard side, else left traffic). The strip icon of a pattern entry draws the same rotated glyph for the aircraft's assigned runway when it resolves at that airport.

  An end is short when its landing distance available is under 1.15 × the physical type's landing distance (`RunwayLandability`, the type from `IMenuAircraft.BaseAircraftType`, not the filed type). LDA is the nav data's declared figure per end (`NavigationDatabase.DeclaredLandingDistanceFt`, AIM 4-3-6.d.3(d)), else the pavement length.

  A short row reads "Runway 28R · short", is drawn at half opacity and stays clickable. A type with no landing-distance figure, a helicopter and a `VEH*` type count as landable. A room token that does not read as a runway is logged once per menu build and names none.
- **The point menu** (a map, taxi-node, runway-threshold or runway-surface right-click with an aircraft selected) opens with the bold title `{callsign} · {type} → {place}`. The place is `HS 30 at W3` at the hold short a threshold click resolves to (`HS 30` when no named taxiway leads to it), `parking {name}`, `spot {name}`, the taxiways meeting at any other node in ordinal order (`B / C`), `runway 28R` on a runway surface, and `this point` on the radar map or at a node no taxiway names.

  On the radar a dimmed row follows the title: `{FRD} · {d} nm, bearing {brg} from the aircraft`, the distance in whole nm (one decimal under 1 nm) and the bearing magnetic as three digits, without the FRD where the fixes cannot name the point. The radar's own FRD row is left out below when this row carries the FRD.

### When a quick command shows

A quick command shows when its flight rules fit, its catalog predicate holds (or a quick-list widening below admits it), it builds an item for the aircraft, and no quick-list rule hides it. The rules read the server's situation flags and `NextCrossingRunway` ([training-hub-contract.md](training-hub-contract.md)) and apply to the quick list only: All Commands keeps every entry whatever they say. They live in `AircraftCommandApplicability` (`Shows*`, `Widens*`) and are applied by `QuickCommandResolver`.

- **Cleared for takeoff** shows while taxiing only within reach of the departure runway's hold line, and at a hold-short only at the departure runway's bar; **Line up and wait** also only at that bar. Both hide while the aircraft is held for release, which the sim refuses.
- **Cross** is named with its runway ("Cross 28R") and sends `CROSS 28R`: at a hold-short the held runway, elsewhere the runway the server reports as next to cross, never the assigned runway. With `ARWY` listing an end of the runway both name that end ("Holding Short 1R" gives `CROSS 1R`; `NextCrossingRunway` is computed with the room's active runways); with none, a hold-short sends the first end the phase name lists and the next-to-cross runway is the end nearest the aircraft.
  - At a hold-short it shows only when the bar is not the departure runway's; **Resume taxi** likewise, since the sim refuses `RES` at the departure runway's bar.
  - While taxiing, holding on the ground (in position, after an exit, after a push) or in a rollout/exit phase (the landing rollout, the runway exit, clearing the runway, a rejected takeoff) it shows only when the next uncleared bar on the taxi route is a runway to cross.
  - On the landing rollout it waits until the rollout decelerates, as Exit left / right do: no taxi instruction immediately after touchdown (7110.65 §3-10-9 note).
  - In a rejected takeoff it waits until the aircraft has slowed to taxi speed (30 kt ground speed or less).
  - No Cross is offered for the next runway while the aircraft is still crossing a runway, or while a runway already cleared to cross lies ahead: one crossing at a time (§3-7-2.c). Leaving the runway the aircraft landed on or is clearing does not count as crossing another.
- **Resume taxi** on a controllable, unheld aircraft in `Taxiing` or a `Following…` phase shows only when `IMenuAircraft.HasTaxiwayHoldShortAhead` is true (the route's next uncleared bar is a taxiway or spot bar, `AircraftCommandApplicability.CanResumeTaxi`), and sends a bare `RES`, which pre-clears that bar. A held aircraft in those phases (a `HOLD` or give-way issued while taxiing or following) is always offered it, and `RES` releases the hold.

  Never where `IMenuAircraft.ResumeTaxiRefused` is true (a `HOLD` broke off a `FOLLOWG` follow and nothing comes after the hold it ends in, held short ahead of it included): `CanResumeTaxi` and `CanResumeFromHoldShort` both hide the row.

  The flag reads the route the aircraft drives, and a `FOLLOWG` follower's driven route carries no hold short, so it is false for followers and the row does not show for one. A held aircraft keeps the stationary-phase rule. `QuickCommandDefaults` lists the row (`MenuIds.GroundResumeTaxi`) in the `Taxiing` situation, second after Hold position.
- **Cancel takeoff clearance** shows only once the aircraft holds a takeoff clearance and is not past V1, where the sim answers "unable" (§3-9-11); it also shows while taxiing with a stored takeoff clearance.
- **Cleared approach** hides once the aircraft holds an approach clearance with descent on it (a JFAC/JLOC lateral intercept still offers it). After a go-around or a missed approach it shows again only once a new altitude is assigned: an approach clearance carries the altitude to maintain until established (§4-8-1).
- **Report field in sight…** hides once the pilot has reported the field in sight (`SituationFlags.HasReportedFieldInSight`, `AircraftCommandApplicability.ShowsReportFieldInSight`) and returns when the flag clears (the pilot loses sight of the field, a go-around off a visual, an approach reset); the server sends the flag for IFR aircraft only, so a VFR aircraft always keeps the entry. It is how to get the report that **Cleared visual** waits for (§7-4-3.a).
- **Cleared to land** hides on final once a landing clearance is on the aircraft.
- **Cleared visual** is offered to IFR aircraft only (`AircraftCommandApplicability.IsIfr`: hidden, not greyed, for VFR; the sim clears only an IFR aircraft for a visual) and opens the visual list (`MenuCatalog.ClearedVisualSpec`, `ApproachVerbSpec.IsVisualList`; titled "Cleared visual approach", described under the approach flyout below) and focuses no row before the field is reported in sight (§7-4-3). The runway its focus opens on defaults as `SmartVisualRunway` chooses it: the assigned runway, else the runway of the active approach, else that of the expected one, each approach id resolved from shorthand (`NavigationDatabase.ResolveApproachId`, so KOAK `R28L` gives 28L and sends `CVA 28L`).
- **Heading seeds are magnetic.** The heading flyout's rose, needle and "now" heading read `IMenuAircraft.MagneticHeadingDegrees` (`HeadingFlyout.PresentHeading`, whole degrees; the rose's keyboard start is that rounded to five), and the Warp flyout's title line and kept heading use it, matching the datablock; `HeadingDegrees` stays the true heading that relative bearings are measured against. The turn row under the point menu's Fly heading reads the same magnetic heading.
- **Assign speed** and **Reduce to final approach speed** hide inside the final approach fix (§5-7-1).
- **Exit left / right** show on the rollout only once it decelerates, and on final only while the server lists the exits ahead (`IMenuAircraft.ExitsAhead` not null). On the rollout, while a list exists, a side shows only with a listed exit on it, since the exit search falls back to the other side when nothing is makeable on the one asked for; with no list (no layout or hold-short data) the side is not judged. On final the side is not judged by the list: the entry stays whenever the runway has exits on that side (`IMenuHost.GetRunwayExits`), and is hidden when it has none.
- **Give way to…** shows only with a taxi route.
- **Air taxi** and **Land** are helicopter-only: `AircraftCommandApplicability.IsHelicopter` reads `IMenuAircraft.Category`, which `AircraftModel` takes from `AircraftCategorization.Categorize` on the aircraft's own type (never the filed one), the test the sim refuses `ATXI` and `LAND` by. A fixed-wing aircraft never sees either, in the quick list or under All Commands, and a type the lookup does not know counts as no helicopter.
  - **Air taxi** (`CanAirTaxi`, `ground.air-taxi`) shows for a controllable helicopter on the ground in the At parking, Holding on ground, Holding short and Rollout / exit situations. In Rollout / exit it waits until the helicopter is settled: on the landing rollout once it decelerates, in a rejected takeoff at or below 30 kt, and always while clearing or exiting the runway (7110.65 §3-10-9.a NOTE). It does not show while Taxiing: an air-taxiing helicopter is airborne and gets Land.
  - **Land** (`CanLandHelicopter`, `ground.land-helicopter`) shows for a controllable airborne helicopter in any situation, an air-taxiing one included, and is the last entry of each such situation's list.
  - **Push back** and **Push back to…** hide from a helicopter's quick list (`AircraftCommandApplicability.ShowsPushback`, in `QuickCommandResolver.Visibility`); All Commands still offers them.
  - Both are catalog entries in the ground-movement block of All Commands (after Preset taxi route) and eligible in Settings › Input › Quick commands. Their glyphs are one helicopter (rotor, body, skids) in the ground colour, Air taxi with a right arrow and Land with a down arrow.
- **Squawk normal** (`ShowsSquawkNormal`) shows first for an aircraft on the ground (`IsOnGround`) whose `IMenuAircraft.TransponderMode` is `Standby`. **Squawk assigned code** (`ShowsSquawkAssigned`) shows first wherever the datablock flags a code mismatch, which is `AircraftCommandApplicability.TryGetSquawkMismatch` ([radar-rendering.md](radar-rendering.md#beacon-code-mismatch-squawk-mismatch)): Mode C, a reported code that differs from the assigned one, a reported code not in 7400/7500/7600/7700/7777, no `SQVFR` latch (`CommandedSquawkVfr`) and an assigned code present. The catalog entry's own predicate (`AircraftCommandApplicability.HasAssignedBeaconCode`) hides it from the Squawk submenu and All Commands for an aircraft with no assigned code (a VFR on 1200), where bare `SQ` would squawk 0000. `IMenuAircraft` carries `TransponderMode`, `BeaconCode`, `AssignedBeaconCode` and `CommandedSquawkVfr` for these. Both are catalog entries too: Squawk assigned code is the first row of the Squawk flyout after the code box in every view's menu and All Commands, and `squawk.assigned` is eligible in Settings › Input › Quick commands. Their glyphs are a transponder box, Squawk normal with a check mark and Squawk assigned code with a return arrow.
- **Climb via SID** shows only with an active SID or one the filed route names at the departure airport (never for a VFR aircraft); **Descend via STAR** only with an active STAR or one the filed route names at the destination. The lookup is the sim's own (`FiledProcedureLookup`), so the menu offers exactly what a bare `CVIA` / `DVIA` accepts.

Quick-list widenings, each first proven against the sim (`QuickCommandSimAcceptanceTests`):

- **Push route…** also while the push is under way.
- **Draw taxi route…** also while lining up, lined up and waiting, on the landing rollout and in the runway exit.
- **Follow…** and **Give way to…** also while already following another aircraft on the ground.
- The VFR **pattern entries**: a downwind or left base also at the start of a go-around or low approach; any leg entry, straight-in final included, also in an airspace-boundary hold or an AR anchor.
- **Cross** and **Cancel takeoff clearance** in the phases listed above.

## The Quick Commands editor

Settings › Input › **Quick commands** (`SettingsSectionId.QuickCommands`) edits the list of every situation but Unknown. The situations are on the left, named by `QuickCommandSituationNames` in enum order; a blue ● marks a staged list that differs from `QuickCommandDefaults`, an amber ⚠ one holding a custom row that fails validation. On the right are a menu preview, the selected situation's rows in order, and the buttons Add command…, Add custom, Reset this situation and Reset all.

- **Staged, then applied.** When Settings opens, every situation's list is copied from `UserPreferences.GetQuickCommandList` into staged rows (`SettingsViewModel.QuickCommands.cs`), and every edit changes only those rows, Reset this situation and Reset all included (both stage the default list, with no confirmation).

  Apply or OK writes each situation whose staged list differs from the stored one through `UserPreferences.SetQuickCommandList`, which removes the stored override when the list equals the default; Cancel discards. The footer's Reset section runs Reset all.
- **What is stored.** Only the situations whose list differs from the default are stored, so improved defaults reach every situation the user never changed. A stored override is removed by storing the default list again through `UserPreferences.SetQuickCommandList`, which is how the editor applies its staged resets.
- **Rows.** A catalog row shows its glyph and label, a flight-rules choice of Default (its catalog rule), Both, IFR only or VFR only, and a remove button. A custom row adds an editable label, a command and an optional ground command, and offers only the last three flight-rules choices.
- **Validation.** A custom row needs a label and a command that prepares as typed text does (`TypedCommandText.TryPrepare` against the staged macros and the staged verb scheme, the same preprocessing the menu applies when it is built); its problem shows under the row. Editing a macro or a verb alias revalidates every custom row. While any situation has an invalid row, `CanApply` is false and OK and Apply stay disabled, with `ApplyBlockedSummary` naming the situations in their tooltip.
- **Preview.** The entry list stays one flat list in drag order, which sets the order within a category and the order of the groups on the strip; it carries no strip divider. The preview draws every row with a glyph through the menu's own factory, `QuickCommandStrip.GlyphCell` (via `GlyphIcon`) laid out by `QuickCommandStrip.Rows`, grouped and labelled by category as the menu draws it, and lists every other row as text. It shows every row, whether or not it would apply to a given aircraft.
- **Show quick command icons.** A checkbox above the intro (`SettingsViewModel.ShowQuickCommandIcons`, staged and applied like the lists; hint "Off: the menu lists its quick commands as text rows instead of icons."), found by Settings search and turned back on by the section's Reset.

  With it off the preview is a vertical list as the menu lays it out: each category's rows under a bold label in category order, custom and uncategorised rows last after a gap.
- **Reorder.** A row is dragged by its ≡ handle (`QuickCommandsSection.axaml.cs`): a move of at least 4 px starts the drag and shows the drop line, Escape cancels it, and the drop calls `SettingsViewModel.MoveQuickCommandEntry(from, insertIndex)`.
- **Add command…** is a flyout over `QuickCommandCatalogOffer`: the eligible catalog actions (`QuickCommandCatalog.Eligible`) the list does not hold yet, filtered by the search text against label or family and grouped by family under `QuickCommandFamilyHeader` rows, with the glyphs drawn by `QuickCommandStrip.GlyphIcon`. Add, Enter or a double-click adds the selection under its catalog flight rules.
- **Search.** Settings search finds the section through the aliases "quick commands", "context menu", "right-click", "icon strip" and "menu commands" (`SettingsSearchCatalog.QuickCommands`).
- **No import or export in the editor.** Quick-command lists are not yet an item type of the Import / Export hub.

## Where it lives

- **One catalog in `Yaat.Client.Core`, behind interfaces** (`IMenuAircraft`, `IMenuHost`, `MenuCatalogEntry`, `MenuIds`), one tree on every surface: radar, ground and the aircraft list build the same menu from the same catalog with no per-surface filtering, and the same view-agnostic builder serves YAAT Scope's aircraft menu. Every entry resolves to command text sent through `IMenuHost.SendAsync`, which `ClientMenuHost` routes to `MainViewModel.SendCommandForViewAsync` on every view, so a failed send shows in the status line wherever the menu opened. A view adds only its view section of canvas items (Display, Draw route), and a point click opens the point menu; both terms are in [`CONTEXT.md`](../CONTEXT.md).
- **Host-answered submenus.** Pushback faces and the point menu's taxi choices are `MenuCommandChoice` lists (label, finished command, optional preview route, children); the point menu's become `PointTaxiFlyout` flyouts. Push back to… is a `PushTargetList` of `MenuPushTarget`s built by `PushbackToMenu.Build`, the one builder for both the All Commands submenu and the strip flyout. Preset taxi route and Taxi to runway are `TaxiRouteRow` lists (below); both draw as designed flyouts, `TaxiRouteFlyout` and `TaxiToRunwayFlyout`.

  Push back to… refills in place while open: a placeholder or the seed first, then the landed plan, with a disabled `Refining targets…` row (`PushbackToMenu.RefiningText`) above the sections while the seed shows. Sections are `Behind the aircraft` (badge TL or TW) and `Taxi spots` (badge S). A target with two or more facings shows `then face` under its name and one chip per cardinal (`PUSH TE FACE N`), each closing the flyout and the menu; one facing is a dim `faces E` note.

  A blocked target's row is enabled but faded, sends nothing and leaves the menu open; `blocked by SWA919` sits on a second line with a `push anyway` chip sending `MenuPushTarget.ForcedCommand` (`PUSHF TF`, `PUSHF $C` for a spot). A chip's accessible name is its command.

  Hold short of… is `IMenuHost.GetHoldShortChoices` → `HoldShortMenu`: the route line (`route S T V W4 · RWY 30`, `route B W · spot 5`), the `MeLine` (`now, taxiing on D`, `now, holding short of 28R on B`; the ` on {taxiway}` tail drops without `CurrentTaxiway`; null in any other phase), one `HoldShortChoice` per bar along the route, nearest first (badge `TW`/`RW`, name, where, distance rounded to 50 ft, `HS` command, preview route, `BarPoint`), and a `HoldShortDestination` (`Runway 30`, `Spot 5`, `Gate B22`, `Helipad H1`, with `route ends here`).

  A bar is a runway bar, a taxiway off the route crossed at a route node, or a route taxiway the route turns onto (the first turn onto each, bare `HS {twy}`, worded "before turning onto {twy}" or "crossing on {X}" when the route meets it earlier; none for the taxiway the aircraft starts on). At one node an off-route crossing sorts before a turn.

  The destination runway whose bar ends the route is that last row, not a stop, and the destination shows only with at least one stop before it. The bar the route starts at is a stop while the aircraft is short of it (no preview route, the box alone); a bar it stands on or has passed is none.

  While the aircraft holds short, the walk starts after the held target's node and never offers the bar it is held at. With no stop the entry is not built (hidden, strip and All Commands).

  `HoldShortLadder` (beside `GroundTrafficFlyout`) is the one builder for the All Commands submenu and the strip flyout, registered through `QuickCommandStrip.FollowInFlyout` as an `IStripFlyoutContent`: a title row (`Hold short of` over the route line), the disabled me row (a teal dot, the callsign, the `MeLine`), the stop rows, then the dimmed, disabled destination row.

  A stop row leads with its bar mark (`HoldShortBarMark`: runway two amber lines, taxiway teal solid over dashed, spot, gate or helipad a teal ring), then the badge, the bold name, the dimmed `· where`, `~N ft` and `HS` command, and sends the command, closing the flyout and the menu.

  Its live refill follows Follow and Give way: in the strip flyout, once a second on `FlyoutLiveTick`; with the pointer outside the ladder is rebuilt as the host answers, with it inside `FrozenStops` holds the order keyed by the `HS` command text, updates distances in place, keeps a stop that left the route as a dimmed `gone` row that sends nothing and the keyboard skips, and appends a new stop at the bottom; the ladder catches up when the pointer leaves. A re-route under a frozen order follows the same rule.

  The first live stop is focused on open; the me and destination rows are disabled and so out of the keyboard's reach. The All Commands submenu refills as it opens, with no live tick.

  A stop pointed at or focused sets a scope preview through a `ScopeFeedbackTracker` built with `highlightsAircraft: false` (it keys rows by their `HS` command and rings no aircraft): `ScopePreview(null, route to the bar, null, BarPoint, null)`, so the ground views box the bar and draw the aircraft's route solid to it, with no route for a zero-segment stop (the box alone). A gone row previews nothing. Preset taxi route and Taxi to runway rows keep `SetRoutePreview`.

  Follow… and Give way to… are one builder each in `GroundTrafficFlyout` (`BuildFollow`, `BuildGiveWay`; `MenuCatalog` calls them), serving the strip flyout and All Commands alike over `IMenuHost.GetGroundTrafficRows` (every other ground aircraft but delayed spawns, nearest first). Both register with `QuickCommandStrip.FollowInFlyout`, so the list is refilled when a surface opens and, while the strip's flyout is open, once a second on `FlyoutLiveTick` (a beat on `IMenuHost.Clock`; a beat queued before the flyout closed or reopened does nothing), an aircraft keeping its row by callsign; a row no longer listed sends nothing, even on a click already on its way.

  The live refill has two modes. With the pointer outside the flyout the list is rebuilt nearest first, `FlyoutItemSync.Sync` adding and removing rows in place. With the pointer inside it (`IStripFlyoutContent.PointerInside`) the order is held (`FrozenTraffic`): each row's distance and state follow the host in place, an aircraft that left stays as a dimmed **gone row** (state `gone`, disabled, skipped by the keyboard, sending nothing), and a newcomer joins the bottom of the list, under the last entry's section in Follow, with a teal left edge (the `newcomer` style class) until the pointer leaves, when the list catches up at once. Give way's gone rows past the first eight are let go while the list is folded, so `Show n more` counts only aircraft still on the ground.

  Give way to… is headed `Nearest first` with the legend `▶ moving  ■ parked`, then one list without live-traffic shadows: each row leads with a ▶ (moving, teal) or ■ (parked, grey) mark, the callsign with the type dimmed beside it, the state under it, the distance and `GW {callsign}`. Eight rows show; `Show n more` shows the rest in place (the row then reads `Show fewer`), and the list stays expanded until it is collapsed or the flyout closes.

  Follow… opens with a filter box (placeholder `Filter or type a callsign`) and a hint line under it, then one scrolling list about eight rows tall under `Moving` and `Parked or holding` titles, nearest first within each, live-traffic shadows included, each row led by Follow's glyph and sending `FOLLOWG {callsign}`. The filter is a substring match on callsign and type, and a section with no match hides. The title of the section scrolled past sticks above the list, and a keyboard-focused row is scrolled into view below it.

  Follow's keyboard is box-first: focus lands in the box, Down moves into the list, Down and Up skip the rows the filter hides, and Enter sends the top match, or the focused row once focus is in the list. With no match, Enter sends `FOLLOWG` with the first word typed and the hint reads `No aircraft matches; Enter sends FOLLOWG N1 as typed.` The box takes focus through `FlyoutBoxFocus.HandFocusToTheBox`, shared with the takeoff flyout's boxes.

  In both flyouts the row under the pointer, else the keyboard-focused row, highlights its aircraft on the scope (`IMenuHost.HighlightAircraft`, the aircraft's datablock on every ground view and every radar view; the radar repaints at once); leaving a row falls back to the focused one, and closing the flyout clears it (`ScopeFeedbackTracker`, which keeps the pointed and the focused row apart). A row that goes gone under the pointer is disabled and raises no `PointerExited`, so its highlight is released when it is disabled and falls to the focused row, or clears.

  The same row also drives a **scope preview** on the ground views (`ScopePreview`, `IMenuHost.SetScopePreview`, fanned over `main.AllGroundViews`; the preview itself draws nothing on the radar): the row's aircraft ringed, the commanded aircraft's remaining route and the row aircraft's, each read through `IMenuHost.GetRemainingTaxiRoute`, and, for Give way to… only, the meet point from `MenuGroundTrafficRow.MeetPoint` (the commanded aircraft's `MeetPoints` entry for that callsign). An aircraft with no route is ringed alone; a gone row previews nothing.

  Paint and draw order are in [ground-rendering.md](ground-rendering.md).

  The preview is sent when the row is pointed at or focused and again with the strip flyout's 1 s list refill, so the routes and the meet point follow the traffic.

  The refill sends it only while the strip's live flyout is open (`FlyoutLiveTick` running), because a submenu closing fires a refill of its own that would put a preview back after the menu cleared it.

  The preview clears with the highlight when the row loses pointer and focus, when the flyout closes and when the menu closes (`AircraftMenuBuilder`, which clears the route preview, the scope preview and the radar cue, on radar and list menus too).

  The radar's feedback is the **radar cue** (`RadarCue(FromCallsign, TargetCallsign, ResolvedCallsign, ShowBearingLabel)`, sent through `IMenuHost.SetRadarCue`, null clears; `ClientMenuHost` sets `RadarViewModel.MenuCue` on every radar view): a solid ring on the aircraft the row names, a dashed bearing line to it from the commanded aircraft labelled `{clock} o'clock · {nm} nm` (unless `ShowBearingLabel` is false), and a dashed ring on the aircraft the call resolves to when that is another one. `RadarCue.ResolveAgainst` places the ends against the aircraft a view draws, so an aircraft the view does not draw gets nothing; drawing is in [radar-rendering.md](radar-rendering.md), step 10.

  A `RadarCueTracker(host, cueFor)` applies the same pointed-over-focused rule as `ScopeFeedbackTracker` for the rows that drive a cue (`Track` per row, `Unpoint` for a row that goes away under the pointer, `Clear` as the flyout closes): it sets the cue and highlights the cue's target. The traffic flyouts build one.

  A Give way to… row whose aircraft has no meet point shows a faint `no conflict predicted` line under its state (`GroundTrafficFlyout.NoConflictPredictedText`), only while the commanded aircraft has a taxi route; a gone row keeps what it said, and Follow… rows never show it. The note means no shared junction within the server's 1,500 ft look-ahead, not that the aircraft will not conflict ([CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md), Meet point).

  A focused row whose aircraft leaves the list, or goes gone while still listed, hands keyboard focus to the live row now at its place, else the one above it; under a Follow filter, the next row the filter shows below it, else above. With no live row left, Give way falls back to `Show n more` and Follow to the filter box. A Follow list whose flyout or submenu is closed leaves its stuck section title alone, and the All Commands Follow… list restates its stuck title when the filter changes.

  With no other ground aircraft the strip's button stays in place, faded and inert (`QuickCommandStrip.MarkInert`), its label in the state line reading `Follow · no other aircraft on the ground`; an open flyout whose list empties shows `No other aircraft on the ground.`; and in All Commands the entry is disabled with the reason beside its label (`MenuDisabledReasonRow`: `Follow… · no other aircraft on the ground`).

  Preset taxi route is `IMenuHost.GetPresetTaxiChoices` → `TaxiRouteRow` (name, taxiways, distance from the aircraft, finished `TAXI` command, resolved path to preview), resolved for the aircraft's own category and wake class from where it starts its taxi; a preset ending where the aircraft stands is left out. `TaxiRouteFlyout.BuildPreset` is the one builder for the All Commands submenu and the strip flyout, registered through `QuickCommandStrip.FollowInFlyout` as an `IStripFlyoutContent`; with no preset the entry is not built.

  Its rows are flat and two-line, nearest first, with no badge: the preset glyph, the name in semibold over the taxiways in the monospace font (`MenuDetailPlacement.Route`), `~N ft` and the `TAXI` command. A row stays within `MenuCommandRow.RowMaxWidth`: its name, taxiway line and command (capped at `MenuCommandRow.RouteCommandMaxWidth`, 180 px) each trim with an ellipsis, and while any is trimmed the row's tooltip shows the whole row.

  Its live refill follows Hold short of…: in the strip flyout, once a second on `FlyoutLiveTick`; with the pointer outside the rows are rebuilt nearest first as the host answers, with it inside `FrozenPresets` holds the order keyed by name and command, updates distances in place, keeps a preset the host stopped returning as a dimmed `gone` row that sends nothing and the keyboard skips, and appends a new preset at the bottom; the list catches up when the pointer leaves. The All Commands submenu refills as it opens, with no live tick. When every preset has gone while open the flyout shows `No preset taxi routes from here.` (`TaxiRouteFlyout.NoPresetsText`).

  The first live row is focused on open; Up and Down skip gone rows and Enter sends. The row under the pointer, else the focused one, previews its route through `SetRoutePreview` (dashed), sent again with each live refill and cleared when the flyout closes.

  Taxi to runway is `IMenuHost.GetTaxiToRunwayChoices` → `TaxiToRunwayMenu`, whose `RunwayEnds` is a `TaxiToRunwayEndList` of every runway end of the airport, each a `TaxiToRunwayEnd` with its role, its state NotSearched, Searching, Ready or Empty, and its rows; `SearchRunwayOf` searches an unsearched runway in the background (see [ground-rendering.md](ground-rendering.md)).

  `TaxiToRunwayFlyout.BuildTaxiToRunway` is the one builder for the All Commands submenu and the strip flyout, registered through `QuickCommandStrip.FollowInFlyout` as an `IStripFlyoutContent`; the host is asked once, when the flyout first opens, and nothing refills while it is open.

  The flyout is a `FlyoutTabStrip` with one tab per runway end of the airport, in the host's order: the assigned end (`30 assigned`), the room's active departure ends (`28L departure`: `Use` Both or Departure, an arrival-only token names none), then the rest by bare designator in runway-number order. The assigned and departure runways are searched when the flyout opens; with no active departure end only the assigned runway is.

  Choosing a tab whose end is not searched starts its runway's search: that tab and the runway's other end show the spinner and the panel reads `Finding runway entries…` (`TaxiToRunwayFlyout.SearchingText`) until the rows land, when the first row takes focus if the flyout is still open and the tab still selected.

  An end with no entry, and one whose search failed (logged as `Taxi to runway: the search of runway {Runway} failed`; arrowing onto the tab or reopening the flyout on it shows the cached `none` without searching, and a left click on it, a UI Automation invoke from another tab, or Enter while it is selected searches once more), is dimmed `none` and its panel reads `No runway entry found` (`NoRunwayEntryText`), as does the flyout of an airport with no runway end at all, which shows no tabs.

  Per end the full-length entry comes first, then every intersection whose runway remaining (the bar's junction with the centreline to the far threshold) is at least the type's takeoff distance (`AircraftProfile.TakeoffDistance`; a type with none keeps the intersections with half the runway ahead), most runway left first. An intersection row shows `~N ft avail`, rounded down to 50 ft; the full-length entry and the nearest usable one are highlighted (`MenuCommandRow.IsHighlighted`, bold).

  An entry is the shortest route to a hold short of the runway. Left out: the hold short the aircraft stands at, a bar reached only over the runway's own centreline (a crossing, not an entry), a far-end bar, a bar on runway pavement only, and a bar whose route passes another hold short of the same runway. An unnamed bar is named by the route's last taxiway (`At W3`).

  Each entry opens For departure and Hold short of runway (`BuildTaxiDestVariants`, with crossing variants when the route crosses a runway; it sends a second-named end and a `1L`-style designator correctly, which also fixes the point menu's variants). The presets that end at the end follow its entries. Routes are cached per start node, category, wake class and runway until the layout changes.

  A tab's panel is its own scroll viewer below the pinned tabs, at most `TaxiToRunwayFlyout.RowsMaxHeight` (420 px) tall and `MenuCommandRow.RowMaxWidth` wide.

  Per entry it lists a title line (`TaxiToRunwayFlyout.TitleText`: `RW At W4 · nearest · ~7,600 ft avail · via S T V W4 · ~2,300 ft`, bold when highlighted), then the entry's variants as `MenuTwoLineRow`s, the label over the whole command in the monospace font, wrapped and never trimmed, a separator between the For departure and Hold short of runway variants.

  Then the end's presets as rows of the same look. Each row's button carries its command (`MenuCommandText`) and its text as its automation name.

  Keyboard focus starts on the first row, so Enter right after opening sends it. Up and Down, and Tab and Shift+Tab, walk the rows, skipping titles and stopping at either end; Left and Right choose the previous or next tab without wrapping and focus its first row. The tabs never take focus, and a tab with no row leaves focus on the panel so the keys keep working. A row sends its command and closes the flyout and the menu.

  A row pointed at or focused previews its route through `SetRoutePreview` (dashed); pointing at a tab draws nothing and leaves the last preview, choosing another tab or closing the flyout clears it. UI Automation sees each tab as a `TabItem` named by `FlyoutTabStrip.AccessibleName` (`28R`, `28R, searching`, `12, none`), which can be invoked to select it.

  The eight approach verbs are one designed flyout, `ApproachFlyout.Build(ApproachVerbSpec, aircraft, context, host)` (strip flyout and All Commands › Approach, registered through `QuickCommandStrip.FollowInFlyout`; `MenuCatalog` routes every `MenuIds.Approach*` verb entry to it).

  `ApproachFlyoutModel` is the data (tabs, `ApproachRow`s, the Force toggle, the box command, the Cancel IFR offer) and `ApproachFlyout` the panel; the approaches come from `ApproachChoices.Choices`, the expected and assigned-runway defaults from `ApproachChoices.Expected` / `AssignedRunwayApproach`, the arrival runways from `IMenuHost.GetActiveArrivalEnds`, and `IMenuHost.IsToweredAirport` says whether the destination has a tower.

  The panel parts it shares with the other flyouts (plain row style, separator) are in `FlyoutPanelParts`.

  A `FlyoutTabStrip` has one tab per runway, labelled `{rwy} {count}`: the room's arrival runways in the room's order, then the rest, and a Circling tab last for approaches that name no runway. The tab with the default approach is selected on open.

  Above the tabs is a pinned default row (`Cleared ILS 30 · default`, `Join straight-in ILS 30 · default`; the label is the spec's) that takes focus on open, so Enter sends it (the expected approach, else an expected visual, else the assigned runway's first approach; without one the default runway's tab, as `SmartVisualRunway` chooses it, else the first, is selected).

  Each tab of Cleared approach and Expect approach starts with a `Visual RWY n` row (none in Cleared for a VFR aircraft; the other six verbs have no visual rows and no airport row), greyed `· field not in sight` until the field is reported in sight, then the runway's approaches by kind. A row on a runway too short for the type (`RunwayLandability`, as above) is dimmed `· short` and stays clickable.

  At an untowered field (Cleared approach, IFR aircraft) a `Visual approach to {airport}` row is pinned beside the default and sends `CVA {airport}`; with no instrument approach there it is the only row and the tabs and box are left out. Cleared approach offers the visual rows to an IFR aircraft only. Rows show the command they send, forced when Force is on.

  Cleared visual approach is the visual list (`ApproachFlyoutModel.BuildVisualList`) under the title "Cleared visual approach", with no tabs, instrument rows, pinned default row or Approach box. Every row is `Visual RWY n` and sends `CVA n`, or `CVAF n` with Force ticked, which also makes every row live without the field reported. The aircraft's default runway comes first, then the room's landing runways in the room's order (`HeadRows`); below them a `Non-arrival runways… · N ›` row (`ApproachFlyoutModel.OtherRunwaysLabel`) unfolds the other runway ends in place (`▾` when open; `OtherRows`) and a second click folds them. `RunwaysFor` says per end whether it is a landing end; a short end is dimmed `· short` and stays clickable. With no landing runway set only the fold row shows and it unfolds every end; with every end landing there is no fold row.

  The default runway's row (`SmartVisualRunway`) is always the list's first row, above the room's landing runways whether or not the room lands on it, and appears nowhere else (`BuildVisualList` puts it at the head of the landing rows and leaves it out of the fold); it has the focus, so Enter clears the visual to the runway the aircraft expects. With no default runway (or none with a visual row) the first landing row has it. Either way only with the field reported in sight (or Force on); otherwise no row is focused and Enter sends nothing. The untowered `Visual approach to {airport}` row and `Cancel IFR…` stay. The entry is disabled with its reason with no destination or no runway data.

  Every approach flyout has a title line naming its verb (`ApproachVerbSpec.Label`). Cleared approach's carries a **Force** toggle, absent in solo training, that turns every row forced (`CVAF`, `CAPPF`); the force pair's rows (`CAPPF`, `JAPPF`) are always forced, and their rows and title carry the force mark.

  The **Approach** box takes an approach id only: Enter sends the top live match on the open tab (`ApproachFlyoutModel.BoxCommand`), or the verb with what was typed when nothing matches. Cleared approach and Expect approach, for an IFR aircraft airborne below FL180, have a `Cancel IFR…` row that opens an inline confirmation strip and sends `CIFR`. Reopening the flyout resets Force, the box, the tab and the visual list's fold.

  Air taxi and Land are one designed flyout, `AirTaxiFlyout.Build(label, AirTaxiVerb, aircraft, context, host)` (strip flyout and All Commands, registered through `QuickCommandStrip.FollowInFlyout`), over `IMenuHost.GetAirTaxiChoices` → `AirTaxiMenu` (`AirportId` and the `Pads`, `Parking`, `Spots` and `Runways` groups of `AirTaxiDestination`s, each nearest first by straight-line distance rounded to 50 ft; `GroundViewModel.GetAirTaxiChoices` answers from the view's layout).

  A `FlyoutTabStrip` has the tabs Pads · parking, Spots and, for Air taxi only, Runways (a group with nothing is a dimmed `none` tab). Above it sits the filter box (Air taxi to, placeholder naming the groups) over a hint line. Each open starts with the box clear and Pads · parking selected, focus in the box.

  Pads · parking lists the true helipad nodes first (a HELI-named parking position stays under parking), then the nearest eight parking positions (`AirTaxiFlyout.ParkingShown`) and an "n more" line. Rows read name, kind, distance and the command: `ATXI @NAME` or `LAND @NAME` for pads and parking, `ATXI $NAME` or `LAND $NAME` for spots. A destination the helicopter stands at on the ground is left out.

  A runway end is one row sending the bare designator (`ATXI 28R`, the full-length bar on the helicopter's side of the runway, which the sim resolves it to), naming that bar's taxiway in its note (`full length at J`), with the end's other bars as chips under it (`ATXI 28R@E`). Two taxiways reaching one bar give one chip, and every chip is one the sim resolves (`GroundCommandHandler.ResolveRunwayHoldShortNode`).

  A bar within 300 ft of the far threshold is left out. A bar the helicopter holds at has no chip; a row whose own bar it holds at reads `RWY 28R · full length at T · holding short here` and sends nothing, only its chips send, and with no chips the row is gone.

  Typing in the box hides the tabs and lists every group's matches by name prefix (a runway end by its designator or its row's taxiway): pads and parking, then spots, then runways, in each an exact name first, then nearest. Enter sends the top match and sends nothing with the box empty or nothing matched. Down moves from the box into the rows and Up from the first row back to it; Left and Right choose the previous or next tab.

  The rows refill once a second on `FlyoutLiveTick`, nearest first with the pointer outside and with the order held and the distances updated in place while it is inside; the All Commands submenu fills as it opens.

  Land's flyout is headed `Land at {AIRPORT}`, the airport its rows come from: the helicopter's destination when the client holds that layout, else the ground view's. When that airport is not the helicopter's filed destination every row and Enter send `APT {AIRPORT}, LAND …`. A Keep NODEL checkbox beside the box appends ` NODEL` to every command, rows and Enter alike.

  Two controls are built for a flyout that shows one runway or group at a time. `FlyoutTabStrip` is hosted by Taxi to runway, Air taxi / Land and the approach flyout; `MenuTwoLineRow` by Taxi to runway and the point menu's taxi flyouts (`PointTaxiFlyout`, which also hosts a `FlyoutTabStrip` for its Taxi to runway). `FlyoutTabStrip` is the tab strip above the rows: a wrapping row of `FlyoutTab`s (label and `FlyoutTabState`: Ready, Searching with a spinner, Empty with a dimmed italic `none`).

  It is passive, its tabs never take focus: the rows panel owns the keys and calls `Move(-1/+1)`, which does not wrap and returns false at either end; a left press or `Select` changes the selection and raises `SelectionChanged` with the new index (`SetTabs` raises none).

  The spinner animation has a finite `IterationCount` because Avalonia's `Animation.RunAsync` faults an infinite one at once; `FlyoutTabStrip.IsSpinning(index)` reports whether that animation is running, for tests, not whether the tab is logically searching (that stays `FlyoutTabState`). `MenuTwoLineRow` (drawn by `MenuTwoLineRowTemplate`) is a row with the glyph and an ellipsized label over the whole command in the monospace font, wrapped and never trimmed.

  The Follow…, Give way to… and Hold short of… rows are not command rows: `GroundTrafficFlyout` and `HoldShortLadder` draw them (above).

  A route row (Preset taxi route) widens up to `MenuCommandRow.RowMaxWidth` (480 px, the width cap of every menu: `App.axaml.cs` `Initialize` overrides Fluent's `FlyoutThemeMaxWidth` with it); the Taxi to runway rows wrap inside it. Past the cap a command row trims its text with an ellipsis and its tooltip shows the whole row; a row that fits has no tooltip. How the rows are found and named is in [ground-rendering.md](ground-rendering.md).
- **Exit left / Exit right are designed flyouts over the named exits ahead** (`ExitFlyout.Build`, one builder for the strip's flyout and All Commands, registered through `FollowInFlyout`), read from `IMenuAircraft.ExitsAhead`; the server's list is never recomputed on the client ([landing-and-runway-exit.md](landing-and-runway-exit.md), Exits ahead). A row is one line: a glyph drawn at the exit's turn-off angle (`ExitFlyout.TurnOffGlyph`, from `ExitAheadDto.TurnOffAngleDeg`), `Taxiway G` with a "· planned" note for the planned exit, its distance and the `EL <twy>` / `ER <twy>` it sends. The tooltip gives the whole command and "turn-off N°". A pick that has become unmakeable is refused by the pilot as for a typed command.

  On the rollout the flyout is a title saying the distances are from the aircraft, the rows, a separator and Pilot's choice (the bare `EL` / `ER`); with no list, Pilot's choice alone. On final it is Pilot's choice, a separator, the rows with their distances "from thr" (the landing threshold; a pattern final rarely has a forecast), a separator and the "Exit at" box.

  On final with no exit forecast on the side, the flyout opens with a dimmed "none forecast" line, no Pilot's choice, a separator and the box. The entry is hidden on final when the runway has no exit on that side, and on the rollout when the list holds none on it.

  The "Exit at" box (`ExitFlyout.BoxName`) suggests from the runway's exits on that side, `IMenuHost.GetRunwayExits` (`GroundViewModel.GetRunwayExits`, which calls `LandingPhase.ListRunwayExits` on the view's layout), listed under it. Ranking: an exact name first, then exits in the aircraft's exits-ahead list in order, then the rest nearest the threshold first; Enter sends the top match. While the box is empty it lists every runway exit on the side. It suggests nothing unless the aircraft's destination is the ground view's airport (`GroundViewModel.LandsAt`; FAA and ICAO forms match, HNL / PHNL). An exit at the threshold (rounded to 0 ft) or behind it is left out whole (KOAK 28R B).

  While the strip's flyout is open the rows refill once a second (`FlyoutLiveTick`). With the pointer outside they follow the list's order; with it inside the order freezes and each distance updates in place. A row the list drops stays as a greyed, disabled row reading "can't make" while its exit is ahead and "passed" once the aircraft has rolled past it (client dead reckoning; on final always "can't make"); a click on it sends nothing and keeps the flyout open. A new exit joins at the bottom, and the list catches up when the pointer leaves. The All Commands submenu refills as it opens.
- **The quick commands** are `QuickCommandDefaults` (the lists), `QuickCommandResolver` (the filters), `QuickCommandGlyphs` (the glyphs and the strip split), `QuickCommandStripLayout` (packs the strip's icons into rows and category segments) and `QuickCommandStrip` (the strip control; its `GlyphIcon`, `GlyphCell` and `Rows` also draw the editor's preview), placed by `AircraftMenuBuilder`. The point menu's title and radar detail row are `SharedMenuGroups.AddPointHeader`, which names a taxi node through `IMenuHost.GetNodeTaxiwayNames` and a hold short through `IMenuHost.GetHoldShortTaxiwayName`. The point menu's strip is `AircraftMenuBuilder.AddPointStrip`, built by `QuickCommandStrip.FromBuilt` from the built point items (the caller bounds the count) with the glyphs in `QuickCommandGlyphs.PointById` / `ForPoint`, kept separate from `ById`; `PointMenuHostCache` wraps the menu host for one point-menu open so the strip and the text items share one set of host answers (taxi choices, runway hold-short targets, the custom-taxi seed).
- **The server classifies the situation.** `SituationClassifier` (`src/Yaat.Sim/Situation/`) stores a snapshotted situation from a sim step with hysteresis bands whose widths the aviation review set, and sends it with one `SituationFlags` wire field and the `NextCrossingRunway` and `ExitsAhead` fields; the client never re-derives them from the phase string.
- **Storage: global per user.** Only the situations a user changed are stored in `preferences.json`, so improved defaults reach users who never customised (`UserPreferences.GetQuickCommandList` / `SetQuickCommandList`; a list equal to the default is not stored). Situations are stored by name; a stored catalog id that no longer exists, or an unknown situation, is dropped at load with a warning. Menus read the lists through `MenuSession.QuickCommandLists` / `MenuContext.QuickCommandListFor`. The file format for a future Import / Export hub item type exists (`QuickCommandListsFile`, `*.yaat-quickcommands.json`), but nothing wires it to the hub yet (YAAT-340).
- **Custom entries.** A list entry is a catalog entry (`CatalogQuickCommandEntry`) or a custom one (`CustomQuickCommandEntry`: label, command text, optional ground text, flight-rules tag).

  A custom entry sends its ground text when the aircraft is on the ground and one is set, else its text, with the callsign added as for every command; the text is prepared as typed text is (`TypedCommandText.TryPrepare`: `!NAME` macros expanded, verbs put in canonical form under the user's scheme) when the menu is built, so a macro or alias edit reaches the next menu opened, and the editor validates the same way.

  The row shows as its alias the first verb of the command its click would send for that aircraft (`SALT` in the air, `TAXI` on the ground), and none for an empty command (`QuickCommandResolver.BuildCustom`). The typed path's other steps do not apply: a partial callsign argument (`FOLLOW AAL1`) is sent unresolved.

  A stored entry that no longer prepares is sent as far as it got, with one client-log warning per entry text per session; it has no glyph, so it always lists as text below the strip.
- **Editor** ([The Quick Commands editor](#the-quick-commands-editor)): `src/Yaat.Client/Views/Settings/QuickCommandsSection.axaml(.cs)` (preview, entry list, drag reorder, the Add command… flyout) and `src/Yaat.Client/ViewModels/SettingsViewModel.QuickCommands.cs` (the staged lists, rows, validation, add/remove/move, resets and Apply).

  In `src/Yaat.Client.Core/ContextMenus/`: `QuickCommandCatalog.cs` (the catalog actions the editor may add, with family, label, default flight rules and glyph) and `QuickCommandSituationNames.cs` (each situation's name in the editor).
