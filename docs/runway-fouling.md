# Runway fouling: which "clear of the runway" each consumer uses

## Sources (read from the local copies under X:/dev/yaat/.claude/reference/faa/)

**P/CG "CLEAR OF THE RUNWAY"** (`7110.65/pcg.md:1468`): the local copy has the heading only; the definition text is missing from the extraction. 7110.65 points to it from 3-9-5, 3-9-6.b, 3-10-3.a.1, 3-10-6 and 3-10-9.b NOTE 1.

The published entry, from the reviewer's own knowledge and not checked against a local file, has three parts. (a) A taxiing aircraft approaching a runway is clear of it when all parts are held short of the applicable holding position marking. (b) A pilot or controller may consider an aircraft exiting or crossing a runway to be clear when all parts are beyond the runway edge and no ATC restriction stops its continued movement beyond that marking. (c) Pilots and controllers use good judgement where edge lines or hold markings are inadequate. The local AIM carries the same substance:

- **AIM 4-3-21.b** (`aim/chap04_sec03.md:530`): "An aircraft is considered clear of the runway when all parts of the aircraft are past the runway edge and there are no restrictions to its continued movement beyond the runway holding position markings. In the absence of ATC instructions, the pilot is expected to taxi clear of the landing runway by taxiing beyond the runway holding position markings associated with the landing runway ... Once all parts of the aircraft have crossed the runway holding position markings, the pilot must hold unless further instructions have been issued by ATC."
- **AIM 2-3-5.a.1** (`aim/chap02_sec03.md:120`): "When instructed by ATC, 'Hold short of Runway XX,' the pilot MUST STOP so that no part of the aircraft extends beyond the runway holding position marking. ... An aircraft exiting a runway is not clear of the runway until all parts of the aircraft have crossed the applicable holding position marking."
- **7110.65 3-10-9.b NOTE 1 / NOTE 2** (`7110.65/chap03_sec10.md:426`, `:433`): the aircraft "should taxi clear of the landing runway by clearing the hold position marking"; "The pilot is responsible for ascertaining when the aircraft is clear of the runway by clearing the runway holding position marking associated with the landing runway." **3-10-9.c**: "Ground control and local control must protect a taxiway/runway/ramp intersection if an aircraft is required to enter that intersection to clear the landing runway."
- **7110.65 3-9-6.b** (`chap03_sec09.md:286`): the departure does not begin takeoff roll until "a preceding landing aircraft is clear of the runway" (REFERENCE P/CG Clear of the Runway). **3-9-5**: "Takeoff clearance need not be withheld until prescribed separation exists if there is a reasonable assurance it will exist when the aircraft starts takeoff roll."
- **7110.65 3-10-3.a.1** (`chap03_sec10.md:83`): the arrival does not cross the landing threshold until "the other aircraft has landed and is clear of the runway" (with the daytime landmark-distance exceptions). **3-10-6.a**: landing clearance "need not be withheld if you observe the positions of the aircraft and determine that prescribed runway separation will exist when the aircraft crosses the landing threshold."
- **7110.65 3-7-4** (`chap03_sec07.md:288`) NOTE: "The operator is responsible for properly positioning the aircraft ... at the appropriate hold line/sign."

**Reading.** The two texts do not conflict; they split by direction. An aircraft approaching a runway is clear only with every part short of the marking (P/CG a, AIM 2-3-5.a.1 first sentence). An aircraft leaving the runway is clear for separation purposes once every part is past the runway edge and nothing stops it from continuing past the marking (P/CG b, AIM 4-3-21.b first sentence). "Not clear until all parts have crossed the marking" (AIM 2-3-5.a.1 last sentence, 3-10-9.b NOTE 2) is the pilot's duty: it is why the pilot keeps moving and only then holds. A leaving aircraft that stops with its tail inside the marking, or that has a hold or a blocked path before the marking, is restricted, so it is not clear under either text. The concern raised is correct for case (b) and only for (b): applying AIM 2-3-5.a.1 alone to a moving exit makes controller-side checks stricter than the P/CG, and it also ignores 3-9-5 / 3-10-6 anticipating separation.

## Two definitions

- **Controller definition (P/CG):** fouls when (i) approaching: any part more than the tolerance past the marking toward the runway; or (ii) leaving: some part still inside the marking and either stopped, or under a restriction to continued movement before the marking (a hold, hold-short, GIVEWAY, conflict yield, route end, or an exit path blocked by traffic). A leaving aircraft moving with no such restriction counts as clear once all parts are past the runway edge (before that it is on the pavement and is already OnSurface or Crossing).
- **Pilot definition (AIM 2-3-5.a.1):** a leaving aircraft has not finished vacating until all parts have crossed the marking.

**Tail-inside occupants.** An exit or `CLRWY` cut back so the nose stops at another runway's bar leaves the tail inside the vacated runway's marking (`RunwayOccupancy.IsTailInsideRunwayState`). It reads as column (c) whether moving or stopped: `RunwayOccupancy.Classify` returns `OnSurface` for a `RunwayExitPhase` with `StopsTailInsideRunwayLine`, a `HoldingAfterExitPhase` with `StoppedInsideHoldingDistance` or `HeldShortOfRunway`, and a `HoldingInPositionPhase` with `TailInsideRunway` (on that runway only). `OccupiedRunwayGoAround` and `SameRunwayArrivalProtection` never project it clear; `RunwaySafetyAdvisor` warns a landing clearance (not a takeoff one) with one on the runway; `GroundConflictDetector` ranks the `CLRWY` hold as vacating.

A crossing that a `HOLD` ends at rest with its tail still inside the crossed runway, and a broken-off `FOLLOWG` follow that rests that way, hold with `HoldingInPositionPhase.AfterFollowBreakOff` and are the same occupant (`TailInsideRunway` names the crossed runway's spoken end: its active end, else the nearest).

A crossing does not set the assigned runway, so `Classify` gives `Fouling` from the aircraft's position, not `OnSurface`; `IsTailInsideRunwayState`, `RunwaySafetyAdvisor` and `GroundConflictDetector`'s vacating rank count it. `RES` stays refused.

The AI callers of `Classify` are `ControllerAi/PositionJurisdiction.cs` and `ControllerAi/Rules/RunwayCrossingGate.cs`; `ControllerAi/RunwayInUse.cs`, `PrecedingDepartureBlock.cs`, `FinalApproachExitForecast.cs` and `Training/SameRunwaySeparation.cs` do not call it. Both callers leave a fouler out: `PositionJurisdiction` does not hand a holder to Tower merely for overrunning the line, and the crossing gate does not close a crossing for one.

## Which runway a phase-driven aircraft is judged against

`RunwayOccupancy.ClassifyByPhase` (and through it `RunwayOccupancy.Classify`, which `RunwaySafetyAdvisor`'s takeoff "traffic on runway" warning calls), `OccupiedRunwayGoAround.UsesThisRunway`, `RunwaySafetyAdvisor.OccupiedRunwayOf` (which feeds its lined-up and line-up-and-wait warnings) and `GroundConflictDetector.PassAirportRank` read `PhaseList.CurrentPhaseRunway`: the departure runway (else the assigned runway) during `LineUpPhase`, `LinedUpAndWaitingPhase`, `TakeoffPhase` and `RejectedTakeoffPhase`, the assigned runway in every other phase.

`PhaseList.DepartureRunway` outlives the takeoff, so an aircraft that took off on 28R for closed traffic on 28L is judged on 28R only while it is lining up, rolling or rejecting, and on its pattern runway 28L once it flies the circuit: it draws no go-around for an arrival on 28R and trips no 28R takeoff warning, and ground-conflict ranking sees it on 28L.

A `HoldingInPositionPhase` runs on no runway of its own (YAAT also uses it as a generic ground-idle hold), so it counts on either its departure or its assigned runway (`PhaseList.RunsOn`) and a pavement test decides: the one in `ClassifyByPhase`, and in `RunwaySafetyAdvisor`'s designator overload the cleared runway's pavement when the designator resolves, else the pavement of whichever of its own runways matches.

## Touch-and-go, stop-and-go and low approach (`ClassifyByPhase`)

7110.65 3-8-2 makes a touch-and-go an arrival until touchdown and a departure after, and a stop-and-go an arrival until its complete stop and a departure after. `ClassifyByPhase` flips both at liftoff as a simplification; its consumers judge the ground interval before it by 3-10-3.a.2 (`SameRunwaySeparation.IsOptionCountedAsDeparting`, `IsOptionOnTheGround`).

| Phase | Kind |
| --- | --- |
| `TouchAndGoPhase`, `StopAndGoPhase` before liftoff | `OnSurface` |
| `TouchAndGoPhase`, `StopAndGoPhase` after liftoff (`HasLiftedOff`) | `Departing`, until the phase hands off at 400 ft AGL |
| `LowApproachPhase` past its assigned runway's landing threshold and below 400 ft AGL | `Departing` |
| `LowApproachPhase` short of the threshold or above 400 ft AGL | none (the phase says nothing) |

Consumers: `RunwaySafetyAdvisor` warns a takeoff behind an option on the ground as traffic on the runway and behind a `Departing` one by 3-9-6.a (an opposite-direction leader reads "cannot begin takeoff roll while that traffic is inbound over the runway").

`OccupiedRunwayGoAround` and the solo evaluator judge the arrival behind a counted-as-departing option by the arrival-behind-departure rule (3-10-3.a.2), and a stop-and-go still braking keeps the landing rule; a restricted low approach over a touch-and-go or a stopped stop-and-go is scored, since 3-10-10 authorizes it only over a landing aircraft; the simulated tower and the AI crossing gate no longer hold the runway as `Departing` for a low approach above 400 ft AGL.

## Implementation notes for the brief

- **Tolerance:** about 5 ft past the marking, measured at the solid (hold-side) line. It has to clear the 2 ft `SetBackStopMarginFt` and nose-on-marking stop noise with margin, so that a correct hold never fouls.
- **"Past the runway edge":** use the tail point (and the wingtips, if the geometry is available) against width/2 plus the existing `LateralSlackFt`, the same test `IsOnPavement` uses.
- **"No restriction to continued movement":** ground speed above `StoppedGroundSpeedKts` (1 kt), and no hold, hold-short, GIVEWAY, active GCD yield or route end located at or before the bar node on its path. An exit path blocked by traffic counts as a restriction. Speed alone is a weaker proxy: an aircraft braking for a yield is still moving.
- **Direction:** "approaching" versus "leaving" comes from the phase (RunwayExitPhase, HoldingAfterExitPhase, or the far side of a CrossingRunwayPhase means leaving), else from whether the heading points toward or away from the runway centreline.
- **Placement:** the controller definition should arrive as a separate `RunwayUseKind` (e.g. `Fouling`), not as `OnSurface`. That keeps RejectedTakeoff and RunwayCrossingGate on their physical rules without special cases, and lets the advisor word its message "past the hold line" rather than "on the runway". The GCD needs its own "still vacating" predicate.

## The marking is a painted segment (YAAT-1214)

"The marking" in the rule below is not a line parallel to the centreline. `RunwayHoldBars.DepthPastMarkingFt` models the paint as a segment through the bar node, square to the bar's own taxiway axis there (`TaxiwayAxisBearingDeg`, straight edges only), half the taxiway width proxy (`HoldShortStandoff.TaxiwayWidthProxyFt`) each side of the node. Each part of the aircraft is judged against the real main gear's position (`aircraft.Position`, the gear), not the body as a whole:

- **In front of the paint** (the part's offset along the line from the node, and the main gear's, are both within half the taxiway width): the part's depth is its perpendicular distance past the line toward the runway, along the taxiway axis.
- **Beside the paint** (a wingtip over the stub's edge, or an aircraft on a parallel taxiway whose wing overhangs the stub): the part is judged against the standoff line parallel to the centreline, S for a bar seated by the rule and `min(S, the bar's distance)` for a bar inside S. A correctly held aircraft at an angled bar is clear (OAK 28L B: wingtip about 283 ft from the centreline, beside the paint), while a part 30 ft toward the runway from the bar fouls.

The 5 ft tolerance, the approaching/leaving split and the restriction test are unchanged. The follower's runway-bar scan (`FollowingPhase`) and `HoldLineFootprint` judge their parts the same way, so a follower stopped at a bar fouls by the same measure as the advisory and the go-around.

## The rule (adopted by the owner, YAAT-402)

An aircraft on the ground but off the runway pavement fouls that runway in two cases. (1) It is approaching the runway and any part of it (the nose, for one facing the runway) is more than about 5 ft past the runway's holding position marking (P/CG "Clear of the Runway" a; AIM 2-3-5.a.1). (2) It is leaving the runway (exiting or crossing) with part of it (the tail) still inside the marking, and it is stopped or restricted short of the marking: under a hold, hold-short, give-way or conflict yield, or its path past the marking is blocked.

A leaving aircraft that is past the runway edge and moving with nothing stopping it short of the marking is clear of the runway (P/CG b; AIM 4-3-21.b), and the current "leaving, stays silent" rule stays for it. A correct hold never fouls. The controller-side consumers use this definition: takeoff and landing advisories (`RunwaySafetyAdvisor`), the occupied-runway go-around (projected to the threshold as today), the solo evaluator (which scores every runway user, a fouler among them, at a takeoff's roll start or a landing's threshold crossing), and the AI's other clearance and separation checks. Two consumers stay physical: the rejected takeoff, which remains pavement-only, and the AI crossing gate, which ignores foulers. Ground-conflict priority uses the pilot rule instead (AIM 2-3-5.a.1; 7110.65 3-10-9.c): a leaving aircraft keeps runway priority until its tail has crossed the marking, and a holder past the line gains none.
